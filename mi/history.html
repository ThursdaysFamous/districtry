districtry / michigan

How this deployment grew

districtry Michigan answers for all 83 counties: the county commissioner districts Michigan's own Bureau of Elections compiles from every county's apportionment filing, plus the national tier every U.S. state can serve from national publishers. It was built dark across three changes in one day — the national tier, the commissioner flagship, then publication — rather than going live on arrival.

The numbers below are measured from the app's own shipped data files every time anything changes — they cannot drift from what the map actually serves. The changelog entries are dated snapshots: each was true on its own date, and none is edited after the fact.

The changelog

  • 5 September 2026

    Grand Rapids, and what a 2018 file had to prove

    Grand Rapids became the second Michigan city this app answers for, and its arrival changed how Detroit is served too: one toggle now covers both cities rather than each getting its own, which is how this project adds places without adding clutter. Links people already shared for Detroit keep working. The city divides itself into three wards and elects two commissioners from each, plus a mayor chosen by the whole city — seven people, and the city says so in its own words on its own page. Six of those seven are published. One ward lists a single commissioner where the others list two, and the city does not say why, so the card tells you a seat is unlisted rather than quietly showing you one name where there should be two. The map behind it needed proving. The city's ward boundaries were last edited in 2018, and a file that old could easily be a map of somewhere that has since been redrawn — the page describing it says it is current, but a description is not evidence. The state of Michigan publishes its own list of voting precincts for the 2026 election, and it records which ward each of Grand Rapids's fifty-nine precincts belongs to. Drawn from that instead, the wards come out in the same three shapes, agreeing almost everywhere: two organisations, working seven years apart, drawing the same lines. So the 2018 date means nothing has changed, and the map is current. That check now runs every time the boundaries are rebuilt, and refuses if the two ever stop agreeing. One thing is stated rather than smoothed over: the wards and the Census Bureau's outline of the city disagree very slightly about where the city edge runs — about twenty blocks and sixty-six people out of nearly two hundred thousand. That is two careful organisations tracing one line, not an error in either, and it is recorded rather than rounded away.

  • 5 September 2026

    Detroit's council districts, and the route that was never walked

    Detroit became the first city this app answers for in Michigan: seven City Council districts, drawn from the city's own published boundaries. Which boundaries took some deciding — the city publishes four competing versions and their names do not settle which is in force, so the choice was made by arithmetic instead. The plan that ships divides Detroit's people almost evenly on the 2020 census; the one it replaced is out by three times as much, which is what a map drawn on the 2010 census looks like ten years later. A fourth version, whose name reads like a rejected draft, turned out to be the very same lines drawn at a coarser resolution, and it is the one that names the dates: the Council adopted this plan on 6 February 2024 and it took effect on 1 January 2026. The card first shipped naming your district and nobody in it, on the finding that every route to the council's membership was closed. Two of those five findings were wrong. The city's open-data portal was called blocked when it answers perfectly well — a conclusion drawn from searching a page for the word "challenge" while the server was plainly saying yes. And the route this project already uses every week for two Illinois counties, reading a public archive of a page whose live version refuses us, was never tried at all. It answers with all nine members. So the card now names your council member, and the two members every Detroiter elects citywide, and tells you which day's archived copy it read. A copy older than 45 days is refused rather than shown to you. No phone number or e-mail appears, and that is the city's doing rather than ours: it publishes none, on the council listing or on any member's own page. That absence is recorded as a gap, in the same place as every other thing this app cannot yet tell you.

  • 3 September 2026

    Published

    Michigan went live at districtry.com/mi/, the fleet's sixth instance. Publishing an instance that had been built in the dark turned up four defects the whole test battery had been green on, and three were the same shape — a claim nothing compared against the thing it claimed. This app's font files had never been copied into its own folder, so all eighteen of them would have 404'd and the page would have quietly rendered in whatever typeface your device chose; its preview image, named in three places, did not exist either. Its sources page still carried Iowa's identity in every tag a search engine reads, because it was cloned from Iowa and only the generated parts were ever regenerated. An unused helper left over from that clone made the fleet's privacy page state that one Michigan layer sends your exact selected point to a government server, which no Michigan layer does. And Michigan's own map bounds, which run out into Lake Michigan because the state's counties do, reached far enough west to claim Chicago. All four are fixed, and two new checks now watch for their kind: every file a page asks for must exist, and every page must say which page it is.

  • 3 September 2026

    County commissioner districts

    The layer Michigan was chosen for: all 619 county commissioner districts across all 83 counties, from the Bureau of Elections' own statewide compilation of the plans counties file under MCL 46.401-46.405. Every Michigan county elects its commissioners by single-member district, so every reader in the state gets a district. The card names the district, its county and the population it was apportioned on — and not the commissioner. The state's layer does carry a name per district, but it lists the winners of the November 2024 election rather than whoever holds the seat now: checked against the counties' own board pages it is 93.5% right, every error runs the same direction, and one district still names a commissioner who died in June 2025. Naming people from it would have been easy and wrong, so this app says what it knows and records the rest as a gap.

  • 3 September 2026

    Arrived

    Four layers, statewide, each with a sourced boundary: county (83), U.S. House (13), Michigan Senate (38) and Michigan House (110). Both chambers and the U.S. House delegation carry a weekly-refreshed roster; the county card names the county and claims nothing about who runs it, because Michigan's commissioner districts ship as their own change rather than half-joined here. The congressional boundary is the first in the fleet built against TIGERweb's 120th-Congress layer — the retired CD119 field now 400s outright.
