Sources & Data Layers Every layer in districtry Michigan, and exactly where its data comes from.

← Back to the map 🧩 What data is missing?

This app is a reader over other people's data. It draws no boundary of its own and names no officeholder it cannot cite, so the useful question about any answer it gives is who published this, and when. That is what this page is for: the publishers the app credits as a whole, then one row per layer naming the source behind that layer specifically.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Always confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.

Data last verified: September 3, 2026

Where a source publishes nothing, the app says so rather than guessing — the missing-data panel on the map is the standing list of what nobody publishes yet, and what would close each gap.

Who publishes the data

The upstream publishers this app depends on, across all layers. Per-layer provenance is in the matrix below.

  • U.S. Census Bureau TIGERweb County and legislative-district boundaries.
  • unitedstates/congress-legislators U.S. House roster (CC0).
  • Open States Michigan Senate and House roster base.
  • Michigan Senate Senate Capitol phone, office and contact page.

The layer matrix

15 layers — 6 political and legislative, 2 public safety, 2 schools, 5 geography and amenities. Each row names the publisher the boundary comes from, where the names on the card come from, and the ground the layer actually answers on — outside that ground the layer hides itself rather than guessing.

County Commissioner Districtcounty-commissioner The county commissioner district covering the point, its county, and the population it was apportioned on.
  • Michigan Department of State, Bureau of Elections — "2021 County Commissioner Districts", the statewide compilation of the plans county apportionment commissions file under MCL 46.401-46.405; all 83 counties in one service, pre-built and shipped with the app. Its AGO item states the licence outright: "This dataset is a public record and ... there are no restrictions on the use, reproduction, or distribution of this dataset." (The Hub SITE item carries a separate CC-BY-SA; that is the site application, not this dataset, and its share-alike is not inherited.) The layer is named for its 2021 adoption year, labelled 2022 by the state's own viewer for the first election held under it, and backed by a feature class called CCD_2022_Edit — three names, one layer.
 NONE, and that is a measurement rather than an omission. The source carries a Commissioner and Party on every one of its 619 polygons, which looks like a free roster and is not one: the item's own description says the values were "derived from the canvased November 2024 election results", so the column is a list of CERTIFIED ELECTION WINNERS, not of officeholders. That is why it is 100% filled — a district always has a winner — and the fill rate is evidence against maintenance, not for it. Measured 2026-09-03 across 12 counties read district-by-district against their own board pages (123 districts) plus ~40 more in a second pass: 93.5% named the right person, and every single miss ran the same direction, the layer naming the 2024 winner and the county naming their replacement. Wayne County District 5 still names a commissioner who died on 10 June 2025. Four further rows misspell a real surname. So the names are dropped at the build rather than shipped with a caveat, and the honest route to Michigan commissioner names — each county's own board page — is a later change with its own weekly refresh. Recorded as gap mi-commissioner-roster. Michigan — statewide, all 83 counties, 619 districts. Every Michigan county elects its commissioners by single-member district (MCL 46.401-46.405); none elects at large, so a polygon is the honest surface everywhere.
City Council Districtcity-ward Which city council or commission district you live in, in the two Michigan cities that publish their own boundaries — Detroit's seven council districts and Grand Rapids's three commission wards — and who represents you in it.
  • City of Detroit — "Detroit City Council Districts 2026", from the city's own ArcGIS org, pre-built and shipped with the app. THE CITY PUBLISHES FOUR COUNCIL-DISTRICT BOUNDARY SERVICES and their names do not settle which is in force: one titled "Current" dating from 2016, one named for 2026, one named for 2013 that was CREATED in December 2025, and NewDistrictBoundariesOption6, which reads like a rejected draft and is the same plan redigitised (99.575% point agreement, measured) — it is the one whose own description names the adoption. The plan was approved by the Council on 2024-02-06 and took effect on 2026-01-01, both stated in this service's own description. The builder decides it by measurement — "Current" carries geometry identical to the 2026 service and different from the 2013 one, and against Census 2020 blocks the shipped plan runs 87,393-94,820 against a 91,302 ideal (worst 4.28%) where the archived plan runs 78,966-100,623 (13.51%), which is what a plan drawn on the 2010 census looks like now. Both comparisons are gates in both directions. The seven districts sum to 639,111 — Detroit's Census 2020 population exactly — so they tile the city with no block double-counted or missed. The item carries no licenceInfo and is shared public by the city.
  • City of Grand Rapids — "Boundaries - City of Grand Rapids Wards" (CGR_Wards), from the city's own ArcGIS org, pre-built and shipped with the app. THE CITY PUBLISHES EIGHT WARD SERVICES AND NONE IS A NEWER PLAN, measured 2026-09-05 by sweeping all 681 of the org's feature services rather than the first 100 (which is how an earlier version of this note said two). Three are 100.000% identical to what ships, one differs only at the perimeter, and three are SINGLE wards rather than rival plans — each agreeing with its own shipped ward. The build gates on the one sibling that has tracked this service byte for byte. THE CURRENCY QUESTION WAS REAL AND IS ANSWERED BY MEASUREMENT: the geometry was last edited 2018-01-24, before the census it is checked against, while the item description claims it is "maintained to reflect the most current adopted ward configuration". Michigan's own 2026 voting-precinct layer assigns each of the city's 59 precincts to a ward (20/20/19), and dissolved by that column it agrees with these polygons on 99.575% of 4,000 points — two independent publishers drawing the same three lines, so 2018 means unchanged rather than stale. That comparison is a gate. Against Census 2020 the three wards run 64,400-68,716 on a 66,328 ideal (worst 3.60%). Unlike Detroit the population identity is NOT exact and is not claimed to be: 21 of 2,883 blocks fall on one side of the ward outline and the other side of the Census place outline, a +66 difference against 198,917, which is two digitisations of one city edge. The item's licence is a use agreement conditioning use on carrying the city's disclaimer; the card quotes the sentences of that agreement that describe the DATA, in the city's own words and order rather than paraphrasing them, with the one cut (its two acceptance sentences, addressed to whoever accesses the data rather than to a reader) marked by an ellipsis. The builder ASSERTS every segment of that quotation against the live licenceInfo on each run, so a reworded agreement fails the build rather than leaving the card quoting words the city has stopped saying.
 The nine members of Detroit's City Council, from the council's own page at detroitmi.gov — seven by district and two at large, per the 2012 charter Art. 4 §4-101. THE LIVE PAGE REFUSES THIS CLIENT: detroitmi.gov sits behind a Cloudflare managed challenge on both the plain and the client-hints rungs, and this project does not defeat a challenge, so the roster is read from the Internet Archive's copy of that same page — the fleet's terminal fetch rung, in weekly use for Kendall and McHenry since 2026-07 — and the card states which day's copy it read. A snapshot older than 45 days is refused rather than served. NO PER-MEMBER phone or e-mail ships: neither the listing nor a member's own profile page carries a single mailto: or tel: link, measured on both, and that absence is the gap detroit-council-contact. The BODY's own office does ship — City Council Office, 2 Woodward Ave. Suite 1340, (313) 224-3443, published once for the whole Council — rendered for the body under the switchboard rule rather than attached to any member. mvic.sos.state.mi.us is challenged the same way; data.detroitmi.gov is NOT (it answers 200 with a readable robots.txt, and an earlier version of this note said otherwise on the strength of one substring match). Detroit's Legistar is abandoned since 2017 and never carried a district in OfficeRecordTitle even while maintained, where Milwaukee's carries "3rd District" in that same field — test a vendor by content per city, never by its name; its Persons table also flags all 45 records active, five of them system accounts. GRAND RAPIDS: the city's own City Commission pages, which are readable — www.grandrapidsmi.gov answers 200 and its robots.txt permits /government/ — so there is no archive hop here. The city states its own arithmetic ("This legislative body consists of the Mayor and six Commissioners... The residents of each Ward directly elect two commissioners"), and publishes SIX of those seven seats: one Ward 1 commissioner where Wards 2 and 3 each have two. THAT SEAT IS VACANT AND THE CITY SAYS SO OFF THE COMMISSION PAGE — its news post of 2026-04-17 states the vacancy "was created following the resignation of former Commissioner Drew Robbins"; an earlier version of this note said the city stated nothing, having measured only the commission page. No city source found states how the vacancy was resolved, so the card names the vacancy and its cause and stops there. The roster carries `seats` and `seatsPerWard` beside its members and a `vacancies` map KEYED BY WARD, so the card accounts for the seventh seat rather than concealing it or inventing a name — the Alexander County machinery, used here for the first time on a districted body. The cause is data, not prose: it was a ward-agnostic string literal in the card at first, on a row that fires for any ward short of its seats, so a later Ward 2 or 3 vacancy would have rendered Ward 1's predecessor by name on the wrong card. A ward that is short with no verified cause gets the neutral sentence instead, which stays true whatever caused it. Each member's own page supplies their ward, e-mail and direct phone; THE E-MAIL DOMAIN IS grcity.us AND NOT THE WEBSITE'S, so a scraper keyed to the site's own hostname finds none. 616.456.3000 is on every member's page and is the city switchboard, DETECTED as the number common to all of them and hoisted to the body rather than hardcoded. Detroit and Grand Rapids only — the two Michigan cities that publish their own council boundaries. Outside both the layer hides itself.
Precinctprecinct The voting precinct covering the point, its county, and how many voters were registered in it for the 2026 cycle.
  • Michigan Dept. of State, Bureau of Elections — "2026 Voting Precincts" (ArcGIS Online item c349bc2bb6c2468594d341a09f520ea8, the CURATED item: it carries the title, the licence, the validation caveat and the full field names). 3,895 features, pre-built by mi/scripts/build_mi_precincts.py (paged, Visvalingam-simplified, 2,000-point agreement gate). THE NEWEST MAP IS NOT ON THE MAPSERVER THAT CARRIES THE COMMISSIONER LAYER: that service publishes a precinct layer per cycle and stops at 2024, while the 2026 map lives on the state's ArcGIS Online org on a different host — and Michigan consolidated 4,340 precincts into 3,895 between the cycles, so building from the MapServer would have shipped a map 445 precincts wrong
 NO OFFICEHOLDER — a precinct elects nobody; it is the unit a ballot is counted in. AND NO POLLING PLACE, which here is a fact about the source rather than a decision: the layer carries no polling field at all, so unlike Iowa's precinct builder there is nothing to decline. The card names the state's own caveat instead — the Bureau collects this from local election officials and says validation was "done by most, but not all" jurisdictions — and points a reader at their clerk. Michigan — statewide, all 3,895 precincts across all 83 counties, as drawn for the 2026 election cycle. Every precinct's county-subdivision key resolves against the Census MCD fabric this instance ships (1,530 of 1,530 distinct keys, measured 2026-09-04 — the same 1,530 on the 2024 map, because the consolidation happened WITHIN municipalities rather than between them), so the precinct and the Township or City card agree on which local government a point sits in. The state's voting-equipment column is deliberately not fetched: it changes on a different clock from the boundary.

Geography & amenities 5 layers

The boundaries that place an address without electing anyone, plus the nearest public services.

LayerWhat it answersBoundary sourceWho it namesWhere it applies
Countycounty Which county contains the point.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — State_County, layer 1, pre-built for the state and shipped with the app
 None yet — identity only. Michigan's county boards of commissioners are elected by district, and the state publishes every county's commissioner districts (with the commissioner's own name and party) as a single statutory filing under MCL 46.404/46.405; that is this instance's flagship layer and ships in its own change rather than being half-joined here. The card names the county and claims nothing about who runs it. Michigan — statewide, all 83 counties.
Township or Citycounty-subdivision The township covering the point, or the Census placeholder record for the city that stands in place of one — and which of the two it is.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — Places / county subdivisions, layer 1, queried live at runtime (no builder script, no committed data/app file); 1,581 features for Michigan, measured 2026-09-04
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, AND THE CARD SAYS SO. Michigan townships elect a supervisor, clerk, treasurer and trustees (MCL 41.61; MCL 42.3 for charter townships), and no statewide roster of them was found in the research pass — recorded as gap mi-township-officers. The card names the office and states plainly that this app does not know who holds it. The 301 city records and the 40 water records are Census bookkeeping constructs, not governments, so no roster claim applies to them at all. Michigan — statewide, all 1,581 records. The charter/general-law distinction is read from TIGER's own NAME suffix rather than a second source, so it cannot go stale against the name printed above it: measured 2026-09-04, the four LSADC values yield exactly four suffixes across 1,581 of 1,581 records.
ZIP Codezip-code The ZIP Code Tabulation Area covering the point — the Census Bureau's areal approximation of the USPS delivery routes sharing a ZIP code, not a postal boundary.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — ZIP Code Tabulation Areas, layer 11 of the PUMA/TAD/TAZ/UGA/ZCTA service, queried live at runtime (no builder script, no committed data/app file). ZCTAs carry NO STATE FIELD because they cross state lines, so this is the one TIGERweb layer here fetched by a bounding ENVELOPE rather than STATE='26' — and the envelope must be Esri's own {xmin,ymin,xmax,ymax} shape, since passing this instance's {minLng,...} METRO_BBOX makes TIGERweb answer HTTP 200 with a JSON error envelope and zero features (measured 2026-09-04)
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, AND NONE IS MISSING: a ZIP code is a mail-routing convenience, not a unit of government, so nobody is elected from one. The card says what the boundary actually is rather than leaving a reader to assume it means more than it does. Michigan — statewide. 1,010 ZCTAs sit east of longitude -87.60; the fetch envelope returns 2,000 because Michigan's bounding box runs west across Lake Michigan to the far end of the Upper Peninsula, and even a clipped box still catches Chicago's 60628 since its south-west corner is in Illinois. NO RECTANGLE ISOLATES MICHIGAN — the same interlocking-states fact metros.json records about the fleet hand-off box — so neighbouring states' ZIPs appear in the map overlay, exactly as they do in Iowa's and Wisconsin's. The CARD is unaffected: it answers from a point query, so a reader always gets the ZCTA they are standing in.
City or Villagemunicipality The incorporated city or village covering the point, if any — and which of the two it is, because in Michigan that changes what else governs the point.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — Places / incorporated places, layer 4, queried live at runtime (no builder script, no committed data/app file); 533 features for Michigan, measured 2026-09-04
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, AND THE CARD SAYS SO — no statewide source names Michigan's mayors, village presidents, council members or clerks; the Michigan Municipal League's directory is membership-facing and the state publishes no aggregate. Recorded as gap mi-municipal-officeholders. Per-municipality rosters are later phase work, the route every other instance took. Michigan — statewide, all 533 incorporated places. A CITY is independent of any township; a VILLAGE remains part of one, so a village resident has two general-purpose local governments and each card names the other. Measured 2026-09-04: all 252 villages land inside a township at their TIGER interior point (235 general-law, 17 charter), zero exceptions, and the Census's own place class codes agree — every village is C1, 'does not serve as a county subdivision equivalent'.

  • USGS The National Map — structures layer 38 (Post Offices), queried live at runtime by a bounding envelope (no builder script, no committed data/app file). Public domain. 1,799 points in Michigan's envelope, every one carrying name, address, city, state and ZIP, measured 2026-09-04. THE FETCH PAGES: the service caps a response at 2,000 records and returns HTTP 200 with exceededTransferLimit rather than an error, so a single request is safe only by margin — Michigan is at 1,799 and its fire-station sibling is at 2,838, already over
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, AND NONE IS MISSING: a post office is a federal facility, not an elected district. The card answers where the nearest ones are, not who runs them. Michigan — plus the border strip its envelope necessarily includes. About this page

This page is generated from the same list of layers the app itself is built from, so a layer cannot ship without a row here and a row here cannot outlive its layer. If a source above has moved, gone dark, or is simply wrong, that is a bug worth reporting.

Found a source the app is missing? Every accepted one is credited by name in SOURCE_CREDITS.md. There's no cash bounty — the credit is the reward.
