# Illinois county build record

Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` (its "What this is" section), where it loaded into every agent session. This is the dated, per-county record of how each Illinois county joined; the rules it produced are in `docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` §3.5.1 and the current state of each county is `docs/COUNTY_STATUS.md`.

**Coverage is no longer "the Chicago collar."** It began as the seven metro counties and grew one contiguous county at a time across central and northern Illinois — north to the Wisconsin line, west to the Mississippi, and south through the Metro East to the Missouri border (**93 counties served, and since 2026-09-06 ALL OF THEM ARE ON THE DISPATCH TIER — the judicial and card-only tiers are empty for the first time, because the statewide library layer reached the last twelve counties that had no county-dispatched layer of their own (it was 79 dispatch / 2 judicial / 10 card as recently as 2026-08-26)**; these numbers move every tranche, so read `DISPATCH_COUNTY_FIPS` and `METRO_COUNTY_FIPS` in `scripts/build_metro_outline.py` as the authority, never this sentence). Jo Daviess, the 63rd, is the first county whose boundary was **PURCHASED**: its GIS office sells data under a signed licence, and the board-district shapefile ships displayed under the county's written permission (licence #008382 + the IT/GIS Director's authorization e-mail), with the raw file retained offline rather than committed — `scripts/build_jodaviess_board_districts.py` records the whole arrangement and the component hashes. COLES, the 64th, retired a gap that was never a block: its site answers HTTP 200 and merely serves an **incomplete TLS chain** (leaf only, no intermediate), which every automated client reports as a failure and no browser notices — so "unreachable" had been recorded where "hand me the issuer" was the whole story, and the scraper now supplies that intermediate by AIA with a pinned hash rather than by disabling verification. Coles also carries the fleet's counter-example to the roster-rides-the-boundary shape: its board layer publishes an `Official` column that is a 2022 snapshot and gets **six of twelve names wrong**, so the app reads geometry from the county's service and people from the county's board page (`scripts/coles_county_board_scraper.py`). CLARK, the 65th, is the first county in the fleet built **entirely out of election returns, with no map at any stage**: its Clerk answered the standing ask with "The County Board is elected by districts. I do not have maps available", which settles the board's form and refuses the geometry in one sentence — but the districts are unions of WHOLE PRECINCTS, so the county's own certified canvasses describe them completely and the Census 2020 voting districts (matching its 23 precinct names 23/23, summing to its exact population) supply the polygons; the roster is those same canvasses' certified winners, so every card row names the election that seated the member rather than claiming a currency the returns cannot show (`scripts/build_clark_boundaries.py`, `scripts/clark_county_board_scraper.py`, and `docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` §3.5.1's canvass route — whose step 4, THE JASPER TEST, is the whole difference between Clark shipping and Jasper still not). That build then found the route is not one county's trick: `il-<county>.pollresults.net` / `.accessliberty.com` is one election-results vendor serving **34 Illinois counties**, publishing each one's certified results as structured data, and sweeping its twelve unserved counties the same day shipped three more — CRAWFORD and MERCER as dissolves of their census voting districts (each walking past a standing obstacle: Crawford's layers wait on a county Mapping Committee, Mercer's only map is a 2021 scan), and MOULTRIE straight onto the County card, its board proven AT LARGE by its own returns. The other nine did not ship and that is the sweep's real yield: each now carries a MEASURED blocker instead of a guess, including both enclaves — Bureau splits sixteen precincts between districts, which closes its last route, and Christian re-precincted after 2020. Twelve counties, nine honest no's, and not one e-mail sent; EDGAR followed the same evening as the fourth, and is the first the route has carried to Clark's full two-witnesses-per-district standard — its Clerk's 2022 General canvass covers all seven districts, with 2024 and the 2026 primary re-tabulating disjoint halves. It also settles where a roster comes from: its canvasses elected a District 6 member who has since left, and only the county's own page names the appointee, so GEOMETRY COMES FROM WHATEVER PROVES THE LINES AND PEOPLE FROM WHATEVER THE COUNTY MAINTAINS AS PEOPLE (`docs/DATA_LAYER_GUIDEBOOK.md`, the two 2026-08-18 entries). FRANKLIN, the 70th, proves the route is not one vendor's either: its Clerk publishes on **platinumelectionresults.com**, a THIRD results vendor carrying eight Illinois counties — two of them still unserved (Marion, Wayne) — and its per-precinct race pages compose the board's three districts from three certified elections that agree exactly, the 2026 primary naming the districts outright where the two generals name only candidates. Two traps are recorded with it: the vendor's precinct ids group in a way that reads like a district key and is not one (Browning 1 and 3 sit in District 1, Browning 2 in District 2), and the county's members page is a GRID whose roles sit in the column BEFORE their own row's name, so a flattened read pairs every chairman with the wrong member (`scripts/build_franklin_boundaries.py`, `scripts/franklin_county_board_scraper.py`). CORRECTED 2026-08-22 — EIGHT WAS THE CURRENT ELECTION'S COUNTY LIST, NOT THE VENDOR'S CARRIAGE: that figure came off the vendor's landing page, which shows only the counties in the election being run right now, while sweeping its HISTORY endpoint by county id answers for SEVENTEEN. Six are unserved frontier counties (Alexander, Fayette, Marion, Union, Wayne, Williamson), and Union and Williamson both publish COUNTYWIDE commissioner contests, which is the County-card shape. Two SERVED counties whose rosters are blocked are carried too — ADAMS (id 54), whose seven-of-21 roster ships from exactly this route as of 2026-08-22, and DEKALB (id 66), one of the three blocked-crawler counties. These are SUMMARY canvasses — they name districts and COUNT precincts, never breaking a contest out per precinct — so the route answers ROSTERS and never composes geometry. That vendor then paid for itself twice more the same afternoon: sweeping its three UNSERVED counties shipped CLINTON as the 71st — five districts whose composition three certified elections agree on, and the tightest population spread this route has produced at 7.3% — and produced two honest no's that are worth as much. MARION is composed completely by its own returns and still cannot be built: five census voting districts under the base names CENTRALIA and SALEM span three board districts each, so no name can place them, where Clinton's one unnameable merge sits wholly inside a single district and therefore cannot move a line. WAYNE had its last open question answered — its certified 2026 primary carries a numbered District 7 contest on Fairfield 1 and Fairfield 2's own ballots — and was still not built, because the composition it confirms runs 1,863 to 3,060 people against a 2,311 ideal, a 32.4% worst deviation beyond the 30% ceiling this project's own dissolve guard enforces (`scripts/build_clinton_boundaries.py`). HENDERSON, THE 92ND (2026-09-14), IS THE FIRST COUNTY JOINED ON A ROUTE THAT READ NOTHING THE COUNTY ITSELF PUBLISHES. Its domain is PARKED (200, a 114-byte `window.location.href="/lander"` body), no results publisher carries its canvasses — pollresults returns the NotFound shell whose md5 is IDENTICAL to a fabricated county name's, accessliberty 404s, GBS does not enumerate it across l_id 1-60, platinum returns no report at any id 1-100 across five election slugs, Clarity 404s — and **ISBE's ByOffice archive carries FEDERAL AND STATEWIDE OFFICES ONLY, no county board contest at any election**, which is why that archive settles precinct NAMES for all 102 counties and can never settle a board's FORM. What it does carry is certified per-precinct results, and Henderson's thirteen precinct names are identical there in the 2022 General, the 2024 General and the 2026 General Primary; the Jasper test passes 13/13 with NO alias and the population identity is exact at 6,387. **THE RECORD THAT KEPT IT DARK WAS ACCURATE WHEN WRITTEN AND WENT STALE INSIDE THIS REPO**: it said "no map data for the county exists anywhere public", which stopped being true on 2026-09-01 when the two McDonough builders began reading `gis.wiu.edu` — the WIU GIS Center, a REGIONAL provider whose `Henderson_basemap_online` service carries parcels, townships, zoning and three emergency-service tilings for Henderson itself. Nobody re-checked the frontier against the new host. **WHEN A NEW SOURCE HOST LANDS, RE-CHECK THE UNSERVED COUNTIES AGAINST IT** (`docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` §3.5.1). That host also supplied a THIRD witness no other county on this route has had — the county's own Townships layer, whose eleven names match the thirteen precincts' base names exactly both ways, Gladstone and Oquawka each split in two — and dissolved to base name its polygons agree with the shipped ones at a MEAN IoU of 0.9977, minimum 0.9961, so the witness covers the GEOMETRY and not only the names. A FULL GIS IS NOT ELECTORAL GEOMETRY and the two are measured separately: that service publishes not one board district or precinct, confirmed against its own layer list, the viewer's config, the server's whole directory and the ArcGIS Online catalogue. Henderson ships PRECINCT-ONLY, the Johnson and Perry shape; its board stays gap `henderson-county-website`, with ISBE's 2007 structure table indicating At-Large with eleven members and NOT PROVEN — that table was measured wrong on Union in exactly this dimension, so no card claims it. It filled its own NOTCH (Mercer, Warren and Hancock all serve, its western edge is the Mississippi), so the ring count is unchanged at six (`scripts/build_vtd_precincts.py`). **Contiguity was retired as a shipping gate on 2026-08-04**: a county now joins whenever a county-keyed layer answers in it, wherever it sits — the served area CAN be disjoint and has been four times (Effingham shipped the same day as that rule, making `metro-outline.json` a MultiPolygon; **ZERO islands as of 2026-08-21 evening**, the file a single Polygon again. Massac islanded that morning — on the Ohio River at the far south of the state, about eighty miles clear of the mainland with all three of its Illinois neighbours (Johnson, Pope, Pulaski) unserved — and JOHNSON ENDED IT the same evening, bordering served Saline on the mainland side and Massac on the island's. Islands can un-island and the dissolve is recomputed, never patched: Hamilton was an island for one day until Jefferson, bordering both it and the mainland, merged it back in, Effingham was one for a week until Shelby did the same, Edwards–Wabash eleven days until White, and Massac for part of one day), and each detached county follows the first-island checklist (`docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` §3.5.1). **The mainland also carries FOUR HOLES — ENCLAVES, the inverse of islands: Bureau County (17011), unserved and entirely enclosed by seven served neighbours (Henry, LaSalle, Lee, Marshall, Putnam, Stark, Whiteside); Christian County (17021), enclosed by four (Sangamon, Macon, Shelby, Montgomery) the day Shelby shipped; and — since RICHLAND shipped on 2026-08-23 — a FIVE-COUNTY hole, the first enclave in the fleet that is not a single county: CLAY, FAYETTE, JASPER, MARION and WAYNE are one contiguous unserved block that used to reach the outside through Richland, and sealing Richland sealed it; and — since VERMILION shipped later the same day — a THREE-COUNTY hole, CHAMPAIGN, FORD and PIATT, which Vermilion's join sealed the same way and which this project PREDICTED WRONG before running `--check`, on the assumption that Ford was an outlet rather than part of the block. Every one of the five is individually MEASURED shut, so it is a hole made of counties that are blocked rather than unexamined, and each carries its own OUTSIDE anchor (Flora, Vandalia, Newton, Salem, Fairfield) so the whole hole is proven unwashed rather than only its rim. THAT JOIN WAS ALSO PREDICTED WRONG BEFORE `--check` WAS RUN: the reasoning said Richland enclosed nothing, which was true of every neighbour taken one at a time and false of the block taken together — **read the ring count from `build_metro_outline.py --check`, never from a map in your head.** **Pulaski very nearly became the third on 2026-08-23 and did not, which is what settles the definition:** Alexander's join left Pulaski with no unserved Illinois neighbour at all (Alexander, Union, Johnson and Massac all serve), the same test Bureau and Christian meet — but the ring count stayed at three, because Pulaski's southern edge IS the Ohio River, the Kentucky state line. A county with no unserved neighbour is an enclave only if it is INTERIOR; one that fronts the state line is a NOTCH in the outer boundary, which the dissolve walks around (the outer ring went from 2,231 vertices to 2,644 in that one change) rather than closing over. **PULASKI THEN FILLED ITS OWN NOTCH WITHIN THE HOUR** and shipped as the 84th, which is the counterpart lesson: nothing new was found and nobody was asked — the vendor holding its answer had been swept ONE ELECTION SLUG WIDE. Re-running that sweep across ten slugs found Pulaski (id 19) and Hardin (id 20) on platinumelectionresults.com, both previously recorded as uncarried, after the Alexander build had already turned up Champaign (2020 only) and Calhoun (2022 only) there. **A vendor's carriage is PER-ELECTION: sweep several slugs before recording that it does not carry a county.** Pulaski's certified 2024 primary settles its board AT LARGE (one countywide commissioner contest per party over all eleven precincts, no district-suffixed contest anywhere, and the only 1-of-1 contests are the eleven committeeperson races), its Jasper test passes 11/11 with the population identity exact, and its precincts ship while its roster does not — the county's site is unreachable and a primary names nominees, not officeholders. The outer ring went back to 2,587 vertices and Mound City moved from OUTSIDE to INSIDE. HARDIN, THE 85TH, FOLLOWED AN HOUR LATER AND CORRECTED A BAR THIS PROJECT HAD JUST INVENTED: Pulaski's own build log said Hardin lacked "a second naming source", a standard Johnson, Perry and Pulaski were never held to — each joined on ONE certified canvass naming its precincts plus the Jasper test, and Hardin's 2026 primary prints a committeeperson contest per precinct per party, twelve contests naming all six and no seventh. Its Jasper test passes 6/6 at 3,649 and its board form now rests on two vendors a decade apart. **Holding one county to a stricter standard than three others is not caution; it keeps a county dark for no stated reason.** Two of its precincts carry an EXPLICIT LABEL rather than a derived one, each with a source: McFarlan from the census's own mixed-case county-subdivision layer (title_case renders "Mcfarlan"), and Cave In Rock from the county's returns where the census hyphenates it. Elizabethtown moved OUTSIDE→INSIDE and Golconda (Pope) took the southern frontier; POPE IS NOW THE ONLY UNSERVED COUNTY IN THE FAR SOUTH. Its record was then re-measured and found wrong in three places: popecountyil.com does not answer 503, it answers 200 with a CONTENT-FREE PLACEHOLDER (stock courthouse hero, a fake 555-555-1212 telephone number, not one county link); popeco.illinois.gov is not "the Clerk's domain" in any web sense — it has NO A RECORD, only a live MX; and the vendor routes are now proven closed rather than unexamined (pollresults returns the fabricated-county md5, accessliberty 404s, the ten-slug platinum sweep of ids 1-100 misses it, GBS and Clarity exclude it). **A MAIL-ONLY `<county>.illinois.gov` IS A DOCUMENTED EXCEPTION CLASS to the Cumberland correction** — Pope, Jasper and Marion all resolve no-A with live MX, so a no-A there means NO SITE HERE and never an unreachable clerk, and all three ask routes are intact. RICHLAND, THE 86TH (2026-08-23), IS THE FIRST COUNTY IN THE FLEET COMPOSED FROM GEOMETRY RATHER THAN FROM ELECTION RETURNS, and the blocker it retired was never a missing source — it was an unfollowed link. Its canvasses COUNT each district's precincts and never name one (the Knox shape), which its gap record had treated as decisive for three weeks; meanwhile `richlandcounty.illinois.gov` links `richlandil.wthgis.com` from its own front page, and that viewer's `Richland_IL` map publishes **`County Board Districtrs` and `Voter Precincts` as two county-authored layers** (7 and 21 features, out of the county's own `17159countyboarddistricts.tml` and `votingprecincts.tml`). Overlaid, every one of the 21 precincts sits 98.4-100% inside a single district, the largest second-district share being 0.89% — digitisation slivers between two independently drawn layers, not splits. **TWO COUNTY-PUBLISHED LAYERS CAN COMPOSE EACH OTHER EVEN WHEN NEITHER IS LABELLED WITH THE OTHER**: neither carries a district attribute on a precinct or a precinct list on a district, and the relationship is in the geometry, so before writing a county off for want of a precinct-to-district TABLE, check whether it publishes the two LAYERS. Three independent witnesses agree — the certified 2024 General counts 4, 4 and 2 precincts in districts 1, 3 and 5 and the composition gives exactly those; the seven populations sum to the county's exact 15,813 across 2,066-2,494 against a 2,259 ideal (worst 10.4%); and the county's raster precinct map agrees everywhere it is legible. **IT ALSO DISPROVED ITS OWN RECORD**, which had read nine of its 21 precincts reporting as two ISBE sub-units each as strong evidence that district lines cut through precincts. They do not, and two benign explanations were tested and BOTH failed: the county sits entirely inside one congressional, senate and house district, so it is not a legislative split, and its unit school districts split two of the nine while also splitting two precincts that are not among them. **What those sub-units are is still unknown and is not guessed at** — a sub-precinct reporting unit is evidence that SOME district line crosses a precinct and never evidence of WHICH, which is why Cumberland's reading was safe there (its board contests over-summed 6+5+3 against 12) and unsafe here. Two traps are recorded with it: the viewer's bulk feature endpoint returns a delimiter-free `<index><id><name>` run in which `Olney Precinct 11020Olney Precinct 10` parses cleanly and wrongly (the first scraper draft returned `Olney Precinct 1` three times and lost 10 and 11), and its DEFAULT layer index (`mid=1`) is not Richland's at all but Dubois County, **Indiana** — the county's own map id comes from the page's `tgisClientInit("Richland_IL")` call. Its roster is the county's own board page, scoped to the roster block because the committees section above it names people who do not sit on the board, and five of the seven members are independently confirmed by certified returns (`scripts/build_richland_boundaries.py`, `scripts/richland_county_board_scraper.py`). The join sealed the five-county Clay/Fayette/Jasper/Marion/Wayne enclave described above and left Lawrence a state-line notch. JACKSON, THE 87TH (2026-08-23), IS THE FIRST COUNTY IN THE FLEET WHOSE DISTRICTS **SPLIT PRECINCTS** AND ARE BUILT ANYWAY. Its own certified 2024 General canvass reports three of its 56 precincts TWICE, under two districts each with their registration divided — Carbondale 21 (D6 600 / D7 282), Carbondale 24 (D5 609 / D6 98), Murphysboro 4 (D3 166 / D5 391) — which is the county stating a district line runs through them, and is why it sat measured-shut beside Bureau, Cumberland, Douglas, Ford, Knox and Piatt. **Its record named the answer and never followed it**: the county publishes its adopted Board District Map as a VECTOR PDF, and the seven districts are seven FILLED PATHS in the content stream — polygon objects whose exact fill colours pair one-for-one with the legend in legend order, each containing its own district numeral. That is the opposite of the colour-sampling the Fayette method rightly forbids: **read the fill OBJECTS, never the pixels.** Georeferenced to EPSG:3436 (which announced itself by matching the aspect ratio to four decimals where Web Mercator, UTM and Albers all missed), the fit lands at a 5.3 m mean boundary offset with the map's own scale bar confirming the scale. **NOTHING TRACED SHIPS**: the 53 unambiguous precincts take their district from the canvass and their geometry from the census voting districts, and the three split ones are resolved to whole census BLOCKS — the map only ever choosing between the two districts the canvass already named. Four verifications, none of them the map checking itself: 53/53 precincts land where the canvass says, 3/3 splits divide between exactly the two districts named, populations run 7,371-7,933 against a 7,568 ideal (worst 4.83%), and Murphysboro 4's population divides 30.2/69.8 where its registration divides 29.8/70.2 — a check **deliberately not claimed** for the two Carbondale precincts, which hold SIU dormitories and student apartments (one block is 700 people in FOUR housing units) where population predicts registration in neither direction. Two silent canvass traps are recorded with it: a row's last two values can share one line, which makes District 6 read as six precincts instead of seven, and the LAST board contest has no next-contest boundary, which makes District 7 read as 77. Both were caught by arithmetic — the distinct precinct count must equal the header's own "56 of 56" and the district registrations must sum to the countywide total — never by eye. The county's roster trap is the same shape: a member's job title is their DISTRICT for twelve of fourteen and their OFFICE for the other two, so the Chair's and Vice Chair's cards name no district and a title-keyed parser ships twelve seats for a fourteen-seat board; **the grouping heading is the authority**, cross-checked against each member's own directory entry. Jackson also FILLED ITS OWN NOTCH — all five Illinois neighbours already served, its western edge the Mississippi — so the ring count is unchanged at four and no hole opened or closed. **FIVE COUNTIES REMAIN SHUT FOR THE SPLIT-PRECINCT REASON and are now worth re-examining rather than re-probing** (Bureau, Cumberland, Douglas, Ford, Piatt): the question is no longer whether their districts are whole-precinct but whether they publish a VECTOR map, and a genuine raster scan is still shut because step one needs path objects (`scripts/build_jackson_boundaries.py`, `scripts/jackson_county_board_scraper.py`). **THAT SWEEP WAS RUN THE SAME DAY AND ITS YIELD WAS ONE COUNTY, REACHED BY A DIFFERENT ROUTE THAN THE ONE BEING TESTED.** DOUGLAS, THE 88TH (2026-08-23), NEEDED NO MAP READ AT ALL: douglascountyil.gov links an ArcGIS web map whose application config names ONE parcel service, while the ORG BEHIND IT (services3.arcgis.com/HXjsHxkFFcZquC8P) carries FIFTY-FOUR — `CountyBoardDistricts` and `VotingDistricts` among them, both shared public with empty licenceInfo. **A VIEWER SHOWS WHAT IT USES; THE ORG SHOWS WHAT THE COUNTY HAS**, which is the Richland finding for a second time and is now the FIRST thing to try, ahead of any map method, because it costs one request and returns geometry rather than an inference. So Douglas ships the county's own polygons AS DRAWN — no dissolve, no georeferencing, nothing derived — even though two of its seventeen precincts (Bourbon 2, Bowdre) are split between districts, which is exactly why nothing else could have drawn it. **THE CHECK IS A DIFFERENT COUNTY PRODUCT**: its certified 2022 General ran all seven districts and printed 2, 2, 3, 2, 3, 2, 5 precincts — nineteen across a county of seventeen, because the two split ones count twice — and overlaying the county's precinct layer on its district layer reproduces all seven exactly, both splits included; that agreement is a GATE in the builder. Its roster is assembled from two documents because the county publishes NO BOARD MEMBERS PAGE: the annual Yearbook names the seven people with a county e-mail each, and the certified summary reports give the district numbers. **THE YEARBOOK'S OWN NUMBERING IS LIST ORDER, NOT DISTRICT** — it lists "1. Six of seven e-mails ship: the yearbook prints District 1's on `douglascountuil.gov`, a typo for the domain the other six use, and a domain check drops it rather than shipping a dead address or silently correcting a character in someone's contact detail. The other four swept counties stay shut and now say why: FORD's maps are all raster (its Patton map is a hand-coloured scan) though its adopted 2021 reapportionment resolution was found and leaves the districts "To be determined" after the COVID census delay; BUREAU publishes no map and references no GIS host at all; CUMBERLAND's only surfaces are parcel and tax products; and PIATT's own GIS asserts "All Rights Reserved", which is the same answer the CCGISC licence gives (`scripts/build_douglas_boundaries.py`, `scripts/douglas_county_board_scraper.py`). VERMILION, THE 89TH (2026-08-23), IS THE BIGGEST COUNTY THE PROJECT HAS ADDED AND THE ONE WHOSE OWN GAP RECORD NAMED THE ANSWER AND WAS NEVER FOLLOWED.
