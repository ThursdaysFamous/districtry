Arkansas sources & data layers

Every layer in districtry Arkansas, and exactly where its data comes from.

← Back to the map 🧩 What data is missing?

This app is a reader over other people's data. It draws no boundary of its own and names no officeholder it cannot cite, so the useful question about any answer it gives is who published this, and when. That is what this page is for: the publishers the app credits as a whole, then one row per layer naming the source behind that layer specifically.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Always confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.

Data last verified: October 13, 2026

Where a source publishes nothing, the app says so rather than guessing — the missing-data panel on the map is the standing list of what nobody publishes yet, and what would close each gap.

This page says where each answer comes from today.

Who publishes the data

The upstream publishers this app depends on, across all layers. Per-layer provenance is in the matrix below.

  • U.S. Census Bureau TIGERweb County, place, school-district, ZIP and legislative-district boundaries.
  • unitedstates/congress-legislators U.S. House roster (CC0).
  • Arkansas State Legislature Arkansas Senate and Arkansas House members, their parties and e-mail addresses, and which seats are vacant.
  • USGS The National Map Police, fire and EMS stations and post offices (public domain).

The layer matrix

18 layers — 7 political and legislative, 3 public safety, 2 schools, 6 geography and amenities. Each row names the publisher the boundary comes from, where the names on the card come from, and the ground the layer actually answers on — outside that ground the layer hides itself rather than guessing.

Districts that elect somebody — from your ward to your seat in Congress.

U.S. Houseus-house The U.S. House district covering the point, and the representative holding it.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — Legislative, layer 0 (120th Congressional Districts), pre-built for the state and shipped with the app; 4 features for Arkansas, measured 2026-10-13. Arkansas did not redraw for 2026: the 120th-Congress map and the 119th's (layer 4) carry the same four districts with identical land areas, every one differing by 0.00000% of its area.

Court of Appeals Districtar-court-of-appeals The Court of Appeals district covering the point: the counties it is made of, and how many of the court's twelve judges its voters elect.
  • Dissolved from the shipped county fabric (TIGERweb State_County layer 1) by ar/scripts/build_ar_court_districts.py, following Act 1812 of 2003's list of each district's counties as the Arkansas GIS Office's metadata for its Appeals Court Districts layer quotes it (read 2026-10-16), and held county by county to that layer. The act was not read at source: the official Code is published by LexisNexis behind a page that needs a browser's scripts, and the two free copies tried refuse this project's client.
  • Arkansas Judiciary — the Court of Appeals' district map page, which lists each district's judgeships (two in Districts 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6, one in Districts 5 and 7)
 No judge is named yet: the court's own pages name every judge, and this app does not publish them yet; recorded as gap ar-appeals-judges. The card says how many of the court's twelve judges the district elects and links the court's district page, which names them. Arkansas — statewide, all 75 counties.
Judicial Circuitar-circuit-court The judicial circuit covering the point: its circuit court, how many judges the circuit's voters elect, and whether a federal court order divides it into election subdistricts.
  • Dissolved from the shipped county fabric (TIGERweb State_County layer 1) by ar/scripts/build_ar_court_districts.py, following the Administrative Office of the Courts' circuit judge directory, which lists each judge under every county the judge serves (read 2026-10-16), and held county by county to the Arkansas GIS Office's Circuit Court Districts layer. The five circuits a federal court order (Hunt v. State of Arkansas) divides into election subdistricts are drawn whole.
  • Arkansas GIS Office — the Circuit Court Districts layer's metadata, which summarises the court order
 No judge is named yet: the directory names every circuit judge, and this app does not publish them yet; recorded as gap ar-circuit-judges. The card gives the circuit's number of judges, from the directory, and links it. Arkansas — statewide, all 75 counties.
Arkansas Senate Districtar-senate The Arkansas Senate district covering the point, and the senator holding it.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — Legislative, layer 1 (state upper chamber), pre-built for the state and shipped with the app; 35 features, measured 2026-10-13. Simplified by Douglas-Peucker at a 20 m interval and refused over a 25 m fidelity ceiling.
 ar-senate-members.json, rebuilt weekly by CI from the Arkansas State Legislature's own member list and each member's own page (name, party, e-mail, the telephone the page prints, which may be the member's own, and the member's page; never the mailing address the page also prints). A member the Legislature's page marks as resigned or deceased is not carried, so a seat with no current member reads as vacant: Senate 1 and 18 on 2026-10-13. Arkansas — statewide, all 35 districts.
Arkansas House Districtar-house The Arkansas House district covering the point, and the representative holding it.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — Legislative, layer 2 (state lower chamber), pre-built for the state and shipped with the app; 100 features, measured 2026-10-13.
 ar-house-members.json, rebuilt weekly by CI from the Arkansas State Legislature's own pages on the same terms as the Senate roster; House 8 read vacant on 2026-10-13. Arkansas — statewide, all 100 districts.
Quorum Court Districtquorum-court The justice of the peace district covering the point, its county, the county judge who presides over the quorum court, and the district's justice where a source says who it is.
  • Arkansas GIS Office — FEATURESERVICES/Boundaries, layer 19 (JUSTICE_PEACE_DISTRICT), credited to the Arkansas Secretary of State: every county's justice of the peace districts in one statewide layer, pre-built and shipped with the app; 775 districts in 75 counties, each county numbered 1 to N at one of the four counts Arkansas Code 14-14-402 allows, measured 2026-10-13. Simplified by Douglas-Peucker at a 20 m interval and refused over a 25 m fidelity ceiling.
 ar-county-officials.json, read on 2026-10-13: each county judge from the Arkansas Judiciary's directory of county judges and clerks, and each county's justices of the peace from the Association of Arkansas Counties' page for that county. A district's justice is named from the county's own site where it numbers them (20 counties, measured 2026-10-13), and elsewhere from the association's 2024 general election results for justices of the peace, updated 17 December 2024, only while the association's current list for the county still includes that winner; a district whose winner has left, or cannot be matched, says so and names no one. Recorded as gap ar-quorum-court. Arkansas — statewide, all 75 counties, 775 districts.
City Council Districtcity-ward The city council ward or district containing the point, in the 12 Arkansas cities above 25,000 people that elect council members by ward, and the members its own voters elect.
  • Arkansas GIS Office — MUNICIPAL_WARDS, layer 50 of its Boundaries service: 852 wards of 262 cities and towns, measured 2026-10-16, of which the 56 wards of these twelve cities are pre-built by ar/scripts/build_ar_city_wards.py and shipped with the app. It carries the Arkansas GIS Office's copyright line, states no licence, and describes itself as not representing exact legal boundaries.
 The members each ward's own voters elect, from the same city council roster the City or Town card reads (ar/data/app/ar-city-councils.json), written down from each city's own pages and proved against them by ar/scripts/build_ar_city_councils.py. Which seats a ward elects was measured from the ballot, and the builder's check holds every drawn ward to a seat its voters elect and every such seat to a drawn ward. The 12 Arkansas cities above 25,000 people that elect council members by ward: Benton, Conway, Fayetteville, Fort Smith, Hot Springs, Little Rock, North Little Rock, Paragould, Pine Bluff, Russellville, Sherwood and Texarkana. The state's map carries wards for 250 more cities and towns. Seven of them are the other cities above 25,000, which elect every seat by the whole city, so a ward there elects no one of its own; the rest are not drawn because this app does not name those councils.

Fire Districtfire-district Every fire district the point is in on the Arkansas GIS Office's map, with the counties each serves, the date the map gives its lines and, for 90 districts, the record the GIS Office holds for them. Where the map labels ground rather than naming a district ("Unserviced" and three others), the card quotes the label.
  • Arkansas GIS Office — FIRE_DISTRICTS, layer 59 of its Boundaries service: the map state law (A.C.A. § 12-10-329, as the layer's own description puts it) has every fire district keep, certified by the mayor, county judge or county fire coordinator, and send to the GIS Office. 971 districts from 973 records, measured 2026-10-16, pre-built by ar/scripts/build_ar_fire_districts.py and shipped with the app. It credits the Arkansas GIS Office and states no licence.
 NO NAMES. The GIS Office's map names each fire district and the counties it serves but not who runs it, and no statewide list of who runs Arkansas's fire districts has been read, so the card names no one rather than guessing; recorded as gap ar-fire-district-boards. Arkansas — statewide. The districts overlap in places and leave less than 1% of the state in no district, so the card lists every district the map puts the point in.
Police Stationpolice-station The three nearest police stations to the point, by straight-line distance, with each one's address.
  • USGS The National Map — structures layer 53 (Police Stations), queried live at runtime by a bounding envelope through the paged loader. Public domain. Measured 2026-10-13: 522 points in Arkansas's envelope, under the service's 2,000-record cap today, and paged anyway so that stops being a fact anyone has to re-check.
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, AND NONE IS MISSING: a police station is a facility, not an elected district. Nobody is elected from one. Arkansas — plus the border strip its envelope necessarily includes. A BUILDING, NOT A JURISDICTION: which agency answers at a given point is a service-area question this instance has not been researched for, so the card says where the nearest are and never who serves you. Nearest-three is a proximity fact, so for a reader near the state line the nearest one really may be in a neighbouring state, and the card's intro says these are straight-line distances.

Schools 2 layers

The district, the attendance zone, and the schools nearest the point.

LayerWhat it answersBoundary sourceWho it namesWhere it applies
School Districtschool-district-unified The public school district covering the point.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — School, layer 0 (unified school districts), copied into a tile archive served from this site, re-fetched from the Census weekly; 234 features for Arkansas, measured 2026-10-13.
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, AND THE CARD NAMES THE DISTRICT ONLY. Arkansas's school boards are elected, and no statewide roster of their members has been established here; the state's school directory lists no board members. Recorded as gap ar-school-board-members. Arkansas — statewide. The label carries no qualifier because there is nothing to distinguish it from: the elementary and secondary tilings TIGERweb serves for other states are EMPTY in Arkansas (0 features each, measured 2026-10-13), and the 234 unified districts' Census land area sums to exactly the 75-county total (134,659 km2).
School Board Zoneschool-board-district The zone a school board elects a member from, and the school district whose board it is. Where a board has resolved to elect every member at large and is changing a seat at a time, the card says when it resolved.
  • Arkansas GIS Office — SCHOOL_BOARD_ZONES, layer 32 of its Boundaries service, which the Arkansas Secretary of State compiles “to aid in election administration and future redistricting”: 965 zones on 176 school districts, measured 2026-10-16, pre-built by ar/scripts/build_ar_school_board_districts.py and shipped with the app. It credits the Secretary of State, is provided as is, and states no licence.
  • Arkansas GIS Office — its notes of 9 April 2026 on the school board zone update, naming the twelve boards that resolved to elect every member at large and when; read through the Internet Archive's copy of 13 May 2026, because the page itself now redirects
 NO NAMES YET. No statewide list of Arkansas's school board members has been found — the Department of Education's report-card feed carries no board field — so the card names no one rather than guessing; recorded as gap ar-school-board-members. The 176 Arkansas school districts whose boards elect some or all members from zones. A board elected wholly at large has no zones, and the empty card says so.

Countycounty Which county contains the point, its county judge, and the justices of the peace on its quorum court.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — State_County, layer 1, pre-built for the state and shipped with the app; 75 features, measured 2026-10-13.

ZIP Codezip-code The ZIP Code Tabulation Area covering the point — the Census Bureau's areal approximation of the USPS delivery routes sharing a ZIP code, not a postal boundary.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — ZIP Code Tabulation Areas, layer 11 of the PUMA/TAD/TAZ/UGA/ZCTA service, copied into a tile archive served from this site, re-fetched from the Census weekly. ZCTAs carry NO STATE FIELD because they cross state lines, so this is the one TIGERweb layer here fetched by a bounding ENVELOPE rather than STATE='05'. 942 features in Arkansas's box, measured 2026-10-13.
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, AND NONE IS MISSING: a ZIP code is a mail-routing convenience, not a unit of government, so nobody is elected from one. The card says what the boundary actually is rather than leaving a reader to assume it means more. Arkansas. The layer is hidden outside the state outline, because outside it the Census's point query answers with a neighbouring state's real ZIP code (the Texas test point gives 75570).
Townshipcounty-subdivision The township containing the point, and its county.
  • Arkansas GIS Office — FEATURESERVICES/Boundaries, layer 57 (TOWNSHIP_POLITICAL_DIVISION), pre-built for the state and shipped with the app (ar/scripts/build_ar_townships.py); 820 townships in 75 counties, measured 2026-10-15. Chosen over the Census's map, which differs from it in at least 27 counties: TIGERweb still draws 38 townships in Washington County, where a 2013 county court order upheld in Clowers v. Edwards (2020) left 3.
 NO OFFICEHOLDER NAMED. A township has no government of its own, but its voters elect a constable for a two-year term (Arkansas Constitution, article 7, section 47). No source naming constables by township has been read, so the card says so and names nobody. Arkansas — statewide.
Voting Precinctvoting-precinct The voting precinct containing the point — where a reader votes and how their ballots are reported.
  • Arkansas GIS Office — ELECTION_PRECINCTS, layer 11 of the Boundaries service, published with the Secretary of State's Elections Division from what each county's voter registration system holds; 2,920 precincts in 2,928 records (a precinct in pieces is joined into one), pre-built for the state by ar/scripts/build_ar_precincts.py and shipped with the app. The GIS Office's web map calls the boundaries a public domain record.
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, and none is missing. A precinct elects nobody — it is an election-administration unit, not a district with a representative — so the card says that rather than leaving a reader to wonder which name is absent. Arkansas — statewide.
City or Townmunicipality The city or town containing the point, if any.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — Places_CouSub_ConCity_SubMCD, layer 4 (incorporated places), copied into a tile archive served from this site, re-fetched from the Census weekly; 501 features for Arkansas (315 cities, 186 towns), measured 2026-10-13.
 In the 19 cities above 25,000 people, the mayor and every council member or director, written down from each city's own pages and proved against those pages by ar/scripts/build_ar_city_councils.py, which fails when a name is no longer on the page it cites (ar/data/app/ar-city-councils.json, 174 seats read 2026-10-17). Where the page a seat cites prints an e-mail address or a telephone number beside that member alone, the card shows it, and the build proves it on that page in the same way; a line or mailbox the page gives the office or several members is not shown, and no street address is. Each seat says whether its ward's own voters or the whole city elect it, measured from the ballot because nothing the state publishes records it. The Arkansas Municipal League's statewide directory is not used: it gives no ward or position and disagreed with five of the twelve cities' own pages where both could be compared. Arkansas's other 482 cities and towns name nobody yet, recorded as gap ar-municipal-officeholders. Arkansas — statewide. A point outside every city and town is unincorporated, which the card states.

About this page

This page is generated from the same list of layers the app itself is built from, so a layer cannot ship without a row here and a row here cannot outlive its layer. If a source above has moved, gone dark, or is simply wrong, that is a bug worth reporting.

Found a source the app is missing? Every accepted one is credited by name in SOURCE_CREDITS.md. There's no cash bounty — the credit is the reward.
