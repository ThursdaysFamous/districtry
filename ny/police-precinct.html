What NYPD precinct am I in?

New York City police precinct lookup — free, by address, ZIP, or a tap on the map.

Every address in New York City sits in one NYPD precinct. The precinct is the command that answers calls on your block, runs the monthly community council meeting, and has a commanding officer you can write to by name.

What the lookup shows

Select any point in the five boroughs and the precinct card names the precinct number, its commanding officer, and the station house that covers it, with a link to the NYPD's own page for that precinct. The station house is dropped on the map as a pin.

Where the NYPD publishes no commander, the card says so rather than guessing.

Precinct, sector, and station house

A precinct is divided into sectors — the patrol areas a neighbourhood coordination officer works. The map has those too: turn the precinct layer on and the sector layer appears under it. A sector has no separate commander, so its card names the sector and its patrol borough and nothing more.

The station house is a building, not a boundary. It is usually inside the precinct it serves, but the precinct is the area and the station house is one address in it.

Where the boundaries and names come from

Precinct boundaries come from NYC Open Data; station house locations from the city's facilities dataset. Commanding officers are re-scraped each week from the NYPD's own precinct pages. The map names no officer it cannot cite. Sources & data layers.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Confirm district assignments with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.
