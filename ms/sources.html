Mississippi sources & data layers

Every layer in districtry Mississippi, and exactly where its data comes from.

← Back to the map 🧩 What data is missing?

This app is a reader over other people's data. It draws no boundary of its own and names no officeholder it cannot cite, so the useful question about any answer it gives is who published this, and when. That is what this page is for: the publishers the app credits as a whole, then one row per layer naming the source behind that layer specifically.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Always confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.

Data last verified: October 15, 2026

Where a source publishes nothing, the app says so rather than guessing — the missing-data panel on the map is the standing list of what nobody publishes yet, and what would close each gap.

This page says where each answer comes from today.

Who publishes the data

The upstream publishers this app depends on, across all layers. Per-layer provenance is in the matrix below.

  • U.S. Census Bureau TIGERweb County, place, school-district, ZIP and legislative-district boundaries.
  • unitedstates/congress-legislators U.S. House roster (CC0).
  • Mississippi Legislature Mississippi Senate and Mississippi House members, their parties and Capitol e-mail addresses, and which seats are vacant.
  • Mississippi Secretary of State Every county's board of supervisors, by district (County Offices and Directory).
  • USGS The National Map Police, fire and EMS stations and post offices (public domain).

The layer matrix

16 layers — 9 political and legislative, 2 public safety, 1 schools, 4 geography and amenities. Each row names the publisher the boundary comes from, where the names on the card come from, and the ground the layer actually answers on — outside that ground the layer hides itself rather than guessing.

County Supervisor Districtcounty-supervisor The supervisor district covering the point, and the supervisor and the election commissioner elected from it, in the counties where this app has confirmed the current district lines. The layer is hidden in the other counties, where the County card still names all five of each.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — tigerWMS_Current, layer 22 (County Subdivisions, January 1, 2026 vintage), where the Census files each supervisor district as a nonfunctioning county subdivision; pre-built and shipped with the app for the 12 counties whose lines the Census changed after the 2020 census and whose five districts hold nearly equal numbers of people on that census's own blocks (3.2% to 9.0% apart, measured 2026-10-15): Choctaw, Clay, DeSoto, Grenada, Lawrence, Lowndes, Neshoba, Noxubee, Oktibbeha, Tate, Webster and Winston. In the other 70 counties the Census still carries older lines, which are never drawn: in 54 of them every resident is in the same district as on the Census's map of January 2010 (measured 2026-10-16). 13 of the 70 are drawn from other published maps instead (below).
  • Central Mississippi Planning and Development District — the supervisor district layers of its county map viewers for Copiah, Hinds, Madison, Rankin, Simpson and Warren counties. Five are named for the plan adopted after the 2020 census (2021 in Rankin, 2022 in Copiah, Madison, Simpson and Warren), and all six give each district's population, which matches the 2020 census's own blocks to the person (in Rankin, after setting aside two blocks the plan leaves out). Redrawn here along whole 2020 census blocks: each block goes to the district that covers most of it, so every line drawn is the Census Bureau's.
  • Covington County — the supervisor district layer of the parcel viewer the county's website links to, where it is called Supervisor Districts 2023, whose 2023 voting precincts each lie inside one of its districts for all but 17 of the county's 18,340 residents in the 2020 census, against 896 for the Census's lines (measured 2026-10-16). Redrawn along whole 2020 census blocks in the same way.
  • Harrison County's own map server — its supervisor district layer, whose service says the lines were approved in 2023 as a result of the 2020 census. The Census's lines there put every resident in the same district as its map of January 2010. Redrawn along whole 2020 census blocks in the same way.
  • Lamar County — the supervisor district layer of the parcel viewer the county's website links to, which matches the printed map of the districts the county publishes, dated 9 August 2022. Redrawn along whole 2020 census blocks in the same way.
  • Lee County — the map published online as Lee Supervisor Districts 2022, which the Lee County Circuit Clerk's 2024 voting precinct map follows: that map names each precinct piece's supervisor district, and agrees with these lines for all but 10 of the county's 83,343 residents in the 2020 census, against 13,351 for the Census's lines (measured 2026-10-16). Redrawn along whole 2020 census blocks in the same way.
  • Pearl River County — the supervisor district layer of the Find My Supervisor map on the county's website, which matches the printed map of the districts effective 1 January 2023 that the county publishes beside it, and the 2023 voting precinct map published with it. Redrawn along whole 2020 census blocks in the same way.
  • Perry County — the supervisor district layer of the Find My Supervisor map on the county's website, whose 2023 voting precincts each lie inside one of its districts for all but 8 of the county's 11,511 residents in the 2020 census, against 444 for the Census's lines (measured 2026-10-16). Redrawn along whole 2020 census blocks in the same way.
  • Tippah County — the map published online as the Tippah County 2022 Redistricting Map, which calls these lines the 2022 Supervisor Districts; who publishes it is not established. The 2024 voting precinct map published as the Tippah County Circuit Clerk Map names each precinct's supervisor district, and agrees with these lines for all of the county's 21,815 residents in the 2020 census and with the Census's lines for all but 669 (measured 2026-10-16). Redrawn along whole 2020 census blocks in the same way.

Police Stationpolice-station The three nearest police stations to the point, by straight-line distance, with each one's address.
  • USGS The National Map — structures layer 53 (Police Stations), queried live at runtime by a bounding envelope through the paged loader. Public domain. Measured 2026-10-15: 462 points in Mississippi's envelope, under the service's 2,000-record cap today, and paged anyway so that stops being a fact anyone has to re-check.
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, AND NONE IS MISSING: a police station is a facility, not an elected district. Nobody is elected from one. Mississippi — plus the border strip its envelope necessarily includes. A BUILDING, NOT A JURISDICTION: which agency answers at a given point is a service-area question this instance has not been researched for, so the card says where the nearest are and never who serves you. Nearest-three is a proximity fact, so for a reader near the state line the nearest one really may be in a neighbouring state, and the card's intro says these are straight-line distances.

School Districtschool-district-unified The public school district covering the point.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — School, layer 0 (unified school districts), queried live at runtime; 137 features for Mississippi, measured 2026-10-15.
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, AND THE CARD NAMES THE DISTRICT ONLY. Mississippi's school boards are elected in most districts and appointed in some, and no statewide roster of their members has been read here. Recorded as gap ms-school-board-members. Mississippi — statewide. The label carries no qualifier because there is nothing to distinguish it from: the elementary and secondary tilings TIGERweb serves for other states are EMPTY in Mississippi (0 features each, measured 2026-10-15), and the 137 unified districts' Census land area sums to exactly the 82-county total (121,535 km2).

 ms-county-officials.json, rebuilt weekly: the five members of each county's board of supervisors and its five election commissioners, by district, from the Mississippi Secretary of State's County Offices and Directory (2026 edition, published 6 May 2026). The board's address and telephone are carried only where at least three of the five supervisors print the same one, because five people do not share a home: 76 counties have a board address and 68 a board telephone on the 2026 edition. A supervisor's own address or telephone is never carried, and for the election commissioners only their names are, because the directory prints each one's own address, which may be a home. Mississippi — statewide, all 82 counties.
ZIP Codezip-code The ZIP Code Tabulation Area covering the point — the Census Bureau's areal approximation of the USPS delivery routes sharing a ZIP code, not a postal boundary.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — ZIP Code Tabulation Areas, layer 11 of the PUMA/TAD/TAZ/UGA/ZCTA service, queried live at runtime. ZCTAs carry NO STATE FIELD because they cross state lines, so this is the one TIGERweb layer here fetched by a bounding ENVELOPE rather than STATE='28'. 827 features in Mississippi's box, measured 2026-10-15.
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, AND NONE IS MISSING: a ZIP code is a mail-routing convenience, not a unit of government, so nobody is elected from one. The card says what the boundary actually is rather than leaving a reader to assume it means more. Mississippi. The layer is hidden outside the state outline, because outside it the Census's point query answers with a neighbouring state's real ZIP code (the Alabama test point gives 36925).

About this page

This page is generated from the same list of layers the app itself is built from, so a layer cannot ship without a row here and a row here cannot outlive its layer. If a source above has moved, gone dark, or is simply wrong, that is a bug worth reporting.

Found a source the app is missing? Every accepted one is credited by name in SOURCE_CREDITS.md. There's no cash bounty — the credit is the reward.
