What people search for6

Google Search Console, the step before a visit, ranked by impressions because that is where the signal is: 192 clicks against 7,030 impressions across 792 distinct searches, 2.73% overall. The pattern is high visibility and low clickthrough, and the top two searches show where that sits: what district am i in converts at 5.9% from an average position of 6.6, while find my district, a near-synonym seen almost as often from position 7.4, converts at 0.7%. Seven results down is the same place on the page for both, so what separates them is the title and description a searcher reads before deciding.

Notes

What each figure above cannot tell you, stated here rather than quietly missing. Every number in these notes is derived from the same data block as the charts.

  1. The two totals. The 3,369 figure is GoatCounter’s own total for the window, and the browser, system and location bars sum to it exactly; the daily columns sum to 3,378, 9 apart. Both count the same period, and which one is larger changes from day to day, so it is not a fixed offset. This page does not know what the difference is, so it prints both rather than deriving one from the other. Counts are GoatCounter “visits”: cookieless, identity-free.
  2. The window, and the root path. This is a rolling two months, July 18 – September 17, 2026, 62 days ending on the last full day. It used to run from launch on 10 July and grow without bound; tracking began that day, so nothing before it exists, and everything before this window does and is simply not drawn here. Until August 24 the Illinois app was served from / at chidistricts.com; that day it moved under /il/ and / became the fleet landing page. The series spans both, so / counts as Illinois on every day before the cutover and as the landing page on the cutover day and after, a whole day at a time, which is the granularity the dashboard publishes per path. August 25 is not a slump. Google Safe Browsing flagged the day-old districtry.com domain that morning, and Chrome shows its warning before the page loads, so blocked visits never reached the counter at all. The flag was a false positive and has since been cleared; the day is under-counted by an unknown amount and cannot be reconstructed. The two Illinois SEO pages the narrative tracks are at 22 visits (/il/police-district.html) and 101 (/il/school-board.html, the third most-visited path on the site).
  3. Why the instance ranking is age. Seven bars: six apps and the fleet landing page. Read the ranking as age, not quality: until August 24 the non-Illinois tags pointed at GoatCounter sites that did not exist and every one of their visits was dropped, unrecoverable. Illinois has the whole window of history; the rest begin at the cutover. Iowa joined the fleet manifest at 01:24 UTC on 28 August, so it is listed on the landing page now; for most of this window it was live and in the sitemap but unlisted, which is how it drew a visit from a page that did not offer it. These seven sum to 1,315 of 1,356 page visits. The split reads the dashboard’s own pages list, which caps at ten rows, so 41 visits on the long tail are in the total and in no bar. A bar reading zero (Michigan) means that path is not among those ten rows, not that the counter recorded none: its visits sit in that long tail, and this page cannot tell how many, zero included. The root path is divided by date rather than guessed at: each day’s root visits go to whichever page was actually there.
  4. Layer ids share one namespace across the fleet, so a layer that exists in more than one city (congress) would sum across them, while city-specific ids (ward = Illinois, council = New York, supervisor-district = San Francisco) are unambiguous. How much blending there could be is measurable even though the blending itself is not: the other five instances have 163 page visits between them against Illinois’s 903, so a shared id could carry at most that share and the Illinois-only ids carry none of it. That ceiling grows as the other instances do, and this note is where it will be visible when it stops being small. The layer list is the top 10 of those recorded.
  5. The referrer list is a top six. GoatCounter’s dashboard shows a “Show more” beyond it that this page cannot query into. The six sum to 2,201 of 3,369 visits, so a named search engine’s true total could run higher than what is shown here. utm_source tags count in the referrer list; the campaigns list counts only visits from links tagged with ?utm_campaign=….
  6. Search counts combine 2 Search Console properties, because the site renamed mid-window and the old domain still appears in results and redirects across; those are two different results for one search, not one counted twice. Google withholds rare queries before anyone sees them, so these will not reconcile exactly with Search Console’s own reports. This is also the one thing the visit counter cannot see: Google strips the query from the referrer, so every one of these searches arrives in the daily chart as an anonymous “Google”. On Bing the figures combine 2 verified properties and the newer one has only just started: districtry.com first appeared in these results on September 13, with 375 impressions against chidistricts.com’s 2,795. Bing is still indexing the rename, which is why the old domain carries most of the figure.

Source: GoatCounter (districtry.goatcounter.com), read-only dashboard export on September 18, 2026. Every app on the domain — Illinois, Iowa, Michigan, New York, San Francisco and Wisconsin — reports to this one site; instances are told apart by path. This report refreshes daily, from three data files fetched on their own schedules and committed to the repository. Tracking is cookieless and identity-free; language collection is disabled by site settings. “Feature interactions” are the app’s custom events: point selects, address searches, geolocation, layer toggles.
