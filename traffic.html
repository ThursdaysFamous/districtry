What the figures say

Searches worth a look

Searches where districtry is on the first page of results, seen at least 100 times, and clicked less than 1% of the time. When the spot is good and the clicks are not, the title and description people see are the likeliest cause.

    Daily activity

    The four days to October 25 ran 922, 993, 833 and 930. Wednesday, Oct 21 is the busiest day in these two months, at 993, tied with Friday, Oct 23.

    Return visits

    134 of 551 map visits since October 22 came from a browser that had opened a districtry map before. The most went to Illinois (27), Wisconsin (24) and South Carolina (15). The count is new, so this share starts low and will rise as more browsers carry the mark.

    Search engines, day by day

    On October 25 search engines sent 298 visits, against 343 the day before and 137 on October 12.

    Google

    How often districtry showed up in Google’s results, and how often someone clicked: 1,025 clicks from 48,738 appearances across 902 different searches, 2.10% overall. what district am i in is clicked 3.6% of the time from an average spot of 6.8 on the page. what district am i in by zip code sits at about the same spot (7.4) and is clicked 3.1% of the time, so the title and description people see likely make the difference.

    Bing

    The same for Bing: 746 clicks from 20,985 appearances across 426 different searches, 3.55% overall. The most-seen search, what district am i in, is clicked 1.1% of the time from an average spot of 5.3. Both engines’ most-seen search is the same phrase. Of the twelve in Bing’s table, zero name a ward or an alderman, against four of Google’s.

    Notes

    What the figures above can’t tell you.

    1. The two totals. The 14,549 total is the visit counter’s own figure. The daily columns add up to 14,719, 170 apart. The gap changes from day to day and its cause is unknown, so both figures are shown. The browser and system charts add up to the total; the country chart is 464 short because it lists only the top entries. The counter uses no cookies and does not identify anyone.
    2. The dates. This page covers the last two months, August 25 – October 25, 2026 (62 days), ending with the last full day. August 25 is low because Google wrongly flagged the new districtry.com address that morning, and Chrome turned visitors away before the page loaded. Those visits can’t be recovered. The Illinois school board page had 345 visits, the fourth most of any page.
    3. Visits by app. Older apps have had more time to collect visits, so read the order as age, not popularity. 58 of 5,512 page visits were to pages that belong to no app, such as the privacy page, so they are in no bar.
    4. Layers. Some layers appear in more than one state’s map, and their counts add up across states. Since September 29 each count also records which app it came from: Congress (Illinois 109, New York 44 and San Francisco 6), County (Arkansas 37, Illinois 114, Indiana 12, Iowa 6, Kentucky 7, Louisiana 1, Michigan 17, Minnesota 12, Mississippi 2, North Carolina 12, Oklahoma 11, South Carolina 4, Tennessee 8 and Wisconsin 14), County board (Illinois 99 and Wisconsin 22), U.S. House (Arkansas 9, Indiana 31, Iowa 9, Kentucky 4, Louisiana 4, Michigan 25, Minnesota 5, Mississippi 1, North Carolina 12, Oklahoma 7, South Carolina 20, Tennessee 22 and Wisconsin 14) and Ward (Illinois 182 and Wisconsin 5). “Not by app” is from before that date, or too small to be listed. The rest belong to one app only: County precinct, IL House, IL Senate, School board and Ward precinct. Only the top ten layers are shown.
    5. Sources. Only the top six sources are shown. They add up to 6,505 of 14,549 visits, so a search engine’s real total may be a little higher. The campaigns list counts only links tagged for a campaign.
    6. Sources day by day. Each day is counted on its own. “Other sites” is any other website that sent a visitor, such as LinkedIn or a news story. “No source” means the browser didn’t say where the visitor came from, such as a typed address or a bookmark; it is in the table but not the chart. A number ending in + means that day’s list was cut short, so the real figure is higher.
    7. Searches. Google and Bing figures each include both districtry.com and the old chidistricts.com, which still shows up in some results and forwards to the new site. Google leaves out rare searches, so these totals won’t match its own reports exactly. The visit counter can’t see what anyone searched for, because Google doesn’t pass that along. On Bing, districtry.com first appeared on September 13.
    8. Shared map links. Since October 17, each Google Maps or Apple Maps link read by a search box is counted once, with which maps app it came from, whether it was pasted or shared in, what the search found, and which map read it. The link and the place are never recorded. A link the front page passes on to a state’s map is counted by both.
    9. Return visits. Since October 22, each state’s map counts a visit once per browser window, as a first visit or a return. A browser that has opened any of the maps before is a return; the mark that says so stays in the browser and is never sent. A cleared browser, a private window or another device counts as a first visit, and every visit before the count began was unmarked, so for its first weeks the share understates how many people come back and will rise as the count ages. The front page and the other pages are not counted here.

    Source: GoatCounter (districtry.goatcounter.com), read-only dashboard export on October 26, 2026. Every app — Arkansas, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, New York, North Carolina, Oklahoma, San Francisco, South Carolina, Tennessee and Wisconsin — reports to this one counter, and each is told apart by its address. Search figures come from Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools. All three are refreshed daily. No cookies are used, no one is identified, and the browser’s language is not collected.
