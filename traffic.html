Daily activity

Every visitor who lands on the site (blue) plus every feature they use — point selections, searches, layer toggles (orange). August 24 — the day the rebrand and the fleet reorganisation shipped — is the single busiest day on record, at 133. August 25 is not a slump. Google Safe Browsing flagged the day-old districtry.com domain that morning, and Chrome shows its warning before the page loads, so blocked visits never reached the counter at all. The flag was a false positive and has since been cleared; the day is under-counted by an unknown amount and cannot be reconstructed. Since then it has settled into a band rather than a spike: 93, 66, 72 and 30 on the four days to August 30, against a pre-rebrand average of 39. The last of those is a Sunday, the quietest weekday here and the first day since the rebrand to fall below that average. The Illinois SEO pages keep compounding — /il/police-district.html is at 18 visits and /il/school-board.html at 14 — and August 27 remains the strongest day since the rebrand at 93.

Six bars: five apps and the fleet landing page. Iowa joined the fleet manifest at 01:24 UTC on 28 August, so it is listed on the landing page now — for most of this window it was live and in the sitemap but unlisted, which is how it drew a visit from a page that did not offer it. Read the ranking as age, not quality: until August 24 the non-Illinois tags pointed at GoatCounter sites that did not exist and every one of their visits was dropped, unrecoverable. Illinois has the whole window of history; the rest begin at the cutover. These six sum to 958 of 958 page visits: each instance is counted from its own path query rather than from one shared top-ten list, which used to drop an instance's sub-pages once that list filled.

What people search for

Google Search Console — the step before a visit. Ranked by impressions, which is where the signal is: 120 clicks against 5,200 impressions across 699 distinct searches, so ranking by clicks would return one real row and then ties.

SearchImpressionsClicks CTRPosition

This is the one thing the visit counter cannot see: Google strips the query from the referrer, so every one of these arrives in the chart above as an anonymous “Google”. The pattern is high visibility and low clickthrough — overall 2.31%, and the single most-seen search, find my district, converts at 0.7% from an average position of 7.4. A position of seven is not a ranking problem; being seen and not chosen is a title-and-description one. Two caveats, both structural. Counts combine 2 Search Console properties, because the site renamed mid-window and the old domain still appears in results and redirects across — those are two different results for one search, not one counted twice. And Google withholds rare queries before anyone sees them, so these will not reconcile exactly with the totals in Search Console's own reports.

Most-toggled layers

Which district layers people actually reach for. Top 10 of those recorded.

The classic Chicago question wins: ward toggles nearly double the runner-up, with state and federal legislative layers close behind. One caveat on these ids: events share a single namespace across the fleet, so a layer that exists in more than one city (congress) would sum across them, while city-specific ids (ward = Illinois, council = New York, supervisor-district = San Francisco) are unambiguous. In this window the blending is still negligible — the other five instances have 21 page visits between them against Illinois's 699 — so these counts are effectively all Illinois. That stops being true as the others grow.
