Who is my Minnesota legislator?

Minnesota Legislature district lookup — free, by address, ZIP, or a tap on the map.

← Back to the map

The Minnesota Legislature has 67 Senate districts and 134 House districts. Each Senate district is divided into two House districts, A and B, and a district can split a county, a city or a ZIP code, so the only way to know which pair covers an address is to put the address on the map. Senators serve four-year terms, with one two-year term each decade so that the whole Senate is elected after the districts are redrawn, and representatives serve two-year terms.

Or leave it blank to open the map and pick your location there.

Find your Minnesota Legislature districts →

Opens the map with both chambers on. Search your address or ZIP, or tap your location.

What the lookup shows

Select any point and the two cards name the district for each chamber and the member who holds that seat, with their party, their St. Paul office and its telephone, their e-mail address where the chamber lists one, and a link to their own page on the chamber's site.

Every other district covering the same point is listed beside them, so one click answers the state question and the county, city and federal ones together.

Two House districts to a Senate district

An address has one district in each chamber, and because every House district is half of a Senate district, the House district decides the Senate one: House districts 14A and 14B together are Senate District 14. The tables below list both chambers in full, so a reader who knows their district can look up the member without opening the map at all.

Where the names come from

Both rosters are re-read on a schedule from each chamber's own published member list and land as a reviewed pull request; the map names no officeholder it cannot cite. A seat the source does not name is shown as unnamed rather than filled in.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.
