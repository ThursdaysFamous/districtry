Minnesota sources & data layers

Every layer in districtry Minnesota, and exactly where its data comes from.

← Back to the map 🧩 What data is missing?

This app is a reader over other people's data. It draws no boundary of its own and names no officeholder it cannot cite, so the useful question about any answer it gives is who published this, and when. That is what this page is for: the publishers the app credits as a whole, then one row per layer naming the source behind that layer specifically.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Always confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.

Data last verified: September 29, 2026

Where a source publishes nothing, the app says so rather than guessing — the missing-data panel on the map is the standing list of what nobody publishes yet, and what would close each gap.

This page says where each answer comes from today, layer by layer: the publisher its boundary is drawn from, where the names on its card come from, and the ground it answers on.

Who publishes the data

The upstream publishers this app depends on, across all layers. Per-layer provenance is in the matrix below.

  • U.S. Census Bureau TIGERweb County, legislative-district, school-district, county-subdivision, place and ZCTA boundaries.
  • unitedstates/congress-legislators U.S. House roster (CC0), including each member's district and Washington offices.
  • USGS The National Map Police station, fire and EMS station and post office points (public domain).
  • Minnesota Senate The member directory the Senate card links while no roster ships.
  • Minnesota House of Representatives The member directory the House card links while no roster ships.

Judicial Districtmn-judicial-district The judicial district covering the point: the district whose trial-court judges the reader votes on, the counties in it, the number of judgeships state law authorizes for it, and where it maintains permanent chambers.
  • Minn. Stat. 2.722 subd. 1 — the statute names the counties in each of the ten districts; the lines are those counties' own, dissolved from the county fabric this app ships, and no new boundary is drawn
  • Minnesota Secretary of State, voting districts — the judicial district on each of the state's 4,105 precincts, read as an independent second witness that every county sits where the statute says
 NO JUDGE IS NAMED, and the reason is measured rather than assumed: www.mncourts.gov refuses all four client rungs this project has with HTTP 403 (measured 2026-10-01), so the 287 authorized judgeships have no reachable publisher here. The card states the authorized count from the statute, says plainly that it names nobody, and links the court. Gap mn-judicial-roster. Minnesota — statewide, all 87 counties.

County Commissioner Districtcounty-commissioner The county commissioner district containing the point: the seat a reader votes on for their county board, which county it belongs to, and how many commissioners that board seats.
  • Office of the Minnesota Secretary of State, Elections Division — Voting Districts, Minnesota: every one of the state's 4,105 precincts carries the commissioner district it sits in, and the districts are those precincts dissolved, so no new line is drawn. Pre-built by mn/scripts/build_mn_commissioner_districts.py.
  • Minn. Stat. 375.01 and 375.025 — the statute read on every run: five commissioners in every county and seven in four named ones, each county divided into as many consecutively numbered districts as it has board members. It is what makes the 447-district partition checkable rather than merely plausible.
  • Seven county governments' own commissioner-district layers (Anoka, Dakota, Isanti, Olmsted, Ramsey, St. Louis and Scott), read as an independent second witness: they agree with the Secretary of State about the district of every one of the 785 precincts they cover. Hennepin's and Sherburne's own layers are published on hosts whose robots.txt refuses this client, which is obeyed.
  • The Secretary of State's certified 2024 and 2022 general-election results, per precinct, carrying the same commissioner-district key as a dated snapshot: the 2024 table agrees with the live fabric about all 3,976 precincts they both name, and the 2022 table about 3,941 of 3,953, the twelve differences being Beltrami and Stevens redistricting after that election.
 The winner of the most recent election for each seat, from the Office of the Minnesota Secretary of State's county races results files (electionresultsfiles.sos.mn.gov), dated to that election: 445 of 447 seats, the 2022 and 2024 generals plus seven special elections. A winner is not a claim about who sits today, and the card says so; five county governments' own district layers are compared each week and a disagreement (Ramsey District 3 today) is printed on the card beside the winner. Built weekly by mn/scripts/build_mn_commissioner_roster.py. Gap mn-county-commissioner-roster. Minnesota — statewide, all 87 counties.

Public safety 2 layers

Who answers a call at this address, and where they answer it from.

Police Stationpolice-station The three nearest police station BUILDINGS to the point, by straight-line distance, with each one's address.
  • USGS The National Map — structures layer 53 (Police Stations), queried live at runtime by a bounding envelope through a paged loader (no builder script, no committed data/app file). Public domain. 694 points in Minnesota's envelope, measured 2026-09-29 — 448 in Minnesota and the rest in Wisconsin (200), South Dakota (24), North Dakota (15), Michigan (4) and Iowa (3), which is deliberate because nearest is a proximity fact. Name, address, city, state and ZIP are populated on every one
 NOT AN OFFICEHOLDER LAYER, AND NOT A JURISDICTION EITHER. The card names buildings and says outright that which agency has jurisdiction at a point — a city police department, a county sheriff or the State Patrol — is a different question that is not published as a boundary this app can read. Minnesota — statewide, plus points across each border, because nearest is a proximity fact.
Fire & EMS Stationfire-station The three nearest fire and EMS station BUILDINGS to the point, by straight-line distance, with each one's address.
  • USGS The National Map — structures layer 51 (Fire Stations/EMS Stations), queried live at runtime by a bounding envelope through the same paged loader. Public domain. 1,484 points in Minnesota's envelope, measured 2026-09-29 — 961 in Minnesota, 376 in Wisconsin, 77 in South Dakota, 50 in North Dakota and 10 each in Iowa and Michigan. THE PAGING IS WHY THIS IS TRUSTWORTHY: the service caps a response and answers HTTP 200 with exceededTransferLimit rather than an error, so a truncated set looks exactly like a complete one
 NOT AN OFFICEHOLDER LAYER, AND NOT A JURISDICTION EITHER. Minnesota fire protection runs on municipal departments and joint-powers contracts, so the nearest station building is not the department that would answer a call, and the card says so. Minnesota — statewide, plus points across each border, because nearest is a proximity fact.

Schools 3 layers

The district, the attendance zone, and the schools nearest the point.

School District (Unified)school-district-unified The unified (every-grade) school district covering the point.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — School, layer 0 (unified school districts), queried live at runtime (no builder script, no committed data/app file); 322 features for Minnesota, measured 2026-09-29
 The winners of each board's most recent elections, from the Office of the Minnesota Secretary of State's school district results files (electionresultsfiles.sos.mn.gov), dated to each election and joined to the Census district through the Minnesota Department of Education's own district layer: 1,882 of 2,099 seats on 326 boards, measured 2026-10-07. A winner is not a claim about who sits today, and the card says so; a seat a special election leaves unsettled names nobody and says why. Built weekly by mn/scripts/build_mn_school_boards.py. Gap mn-school-board-members. Minnesota — statewide.
School District (Elementary)school-district-elementary The elementary-tiling school district covering the point, in the eight places Minnesota has one.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — School, layer 2 (elementary school districts), queried live at runtime (no builder script, no committed data/app file); 8 features for Minnesota, measured 2026-09-29. SEVEN OF THE EIGHT record LOGRADE PK and HIGRADE 12, the same span as a unified district, so the card prints the recorded grades and the tiling and claims nothing about which grades the district operates — recorded as the coverage gap mn-elementary-district-grades
 The same school board roster as the unified layer's row, keyed by this layer's Census district id. Gap mn-school-board-members. Minnesota — the eight districts the Census places in this tiling; the card is correctly empty everywhere else.
School District (Secondary)school-district-secondary The secondary school district covering the point, in the one place Minnesota has one.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — School, layer 1 (secondary school districts), queried live at runtime (no builder script, no committed data/app file); exactly 1 feature for Minnesota, measured 2026-09-29 — 'Park Rapids Public School District in Pine Point (9-12)'. It ships rather than being dropped because it IS the answer where it applies: without it a reader in Pine Point would be shown an elementary district and no high-school district at all
 NOBODY IS NAMED HERE, deliberately: the one secondary district (Park Rapids, grades 9-12) lies over the Pine Point district, whose voters elect Pine Point's own board, so naming Park Rapids's board on this card would name people a reader there does not vote for. The Elementary card is the one for Pine Point. Minnesota — the single district the Census publishes in this tiling; the card is correctly empty everywhere else.

Geography & amenities 7 layers

The boundaries that place an address without electing anyone, plus the nearest public services.

LayerWhat it answersBoundary sourceWho it namesWhere it applies
Countycounty Which county contains the point.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — State_County, layer 1, pre-built for the state and shipped with the app
 Not yet shipped. Minnesota's 87 county boards are elected by district (447 districts), and the districts and their members arrive together in a later change; the card names no officeholder rather than guessing one. Recorded as the coverage gap mn-county-commissioner. Minnesota — statewide.
Watershed Districtwatershed-district The watershed district or watershed management organization covering the point: what kind of body it is, the state law that creates it, who appoints its board, and its own website. Where the state's map puts the point in two, both are listed.
  • Minnesota Board of Water and Soil Resources (BWSR), Watershed Management Districts and Organizations — 45 watershed districts, 16 watershed management organizations and three county-run areas, read from the state's feature service and simplified by mn/scripts/build_mn_watershed_districts.py (Douglas-Peucker at 10 m, gated on 4,000 sampled points getting the same bodies as the source). The Geospatial Commons download host refuses this client in robots.txt and is not read
 NO MANAGER IS NAMED, because no board here is elected: a watershed district's managers are appointed by the county boards of the counties it lies in (Minn. Stat. 103D.311), a metro organization's board by its member cities and towns (103B.211), and a county-run area is the county board itself. The card says who appoints the board and links the body's own website as BWSR publishes it. Minnesota — the parts of the state inside a watershed district or organization, 30% of its area: 99.7-99.9% of each of the seven Twin Cities counties (measured 2026-10-06 against the shipped county outlines), and wherever a district was petitioned for elsewhere.
ZIP Codezip-code The ZIP Code Tabulation Area covering the point — the Census Bureau's areal approximation of the delivery routes sharing a ZIP code, not a postal boundary.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — ZIP Code Tabulation Areas, layer 11 of the PUMA/TAD/TAZ/UGA/ZCTA service, queried live at runtime (no builder script, no committed data/app file). A ZCTA has NO STATE FIELD because it can cross a state line, so this is the one TIGERweb layer here fetched by a bounding ENVELOPE rather than STATE='27'; 1,385 features in Minnesota's envelope, measured 2026-09-29
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, AND THE CARD SAYS SO IN AS MANY WORDS: a ZIP code elects nobody. It is a postal delivery grouping the Census approximates as an area, and the card states that rather than leaving a reader to wonder why no name appears. Minnesota — statewide.
Township or Citycounty-subdivision The organized town covering the point, or the Census placeholder record for the city that stands in place of one — and which of the two it is.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — Places / county subdivisions, layer 1, queried live at runtime (no builder script, no committed data/app file); 2,762 features for Minnesota, measured 2026-09-29
 NO OFFICEHOLDER. A Minnesota town elects three supervisors to three-year terms plus a clerk and a treasurer in alternate years (Minn. Stat. 367.03, read at revisor.mn.gov on 2026-09-29), and no statewide roster of those officers was found, so the card names nobody. Recorded as the coverage gap mn-township-officers. A city's people belong on the City card and an unorganized territory's on the county's, so neither gets a town-board row at all. Minnesota — statewide.
Voting Precinctvoting-precinct The voting precinct containing the point — where a reader votes and how their ballots are reported.
  • Office of the Minnesota Secretary of State, Elections Division — Voting Districts, Minnesota, served through MnGeo's enterprise ArcGIS host; 4,105 features, pre-built for the state by mn/scripts/build_mn_precincts.py and shipped with the app. The office states it cannot assure the lines are exact and directs anyone needing a definitive one to the city, township, or county, which the card says.
 NO OFFICEHOLDER, and none is missing. A precinct elects nobody — it is an election-administration unit, not a district with a representative — so the card says that rather than leaving a reader to wonder which name is absent. Minnesota — statewide.
Citymunicipality The city covering the point, if any. Most of Minnesota's land is township or unorganized territory, where the card is correctly empty.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb — Places / incorporated places, layer 4, queried live at runtime (no builder script, no committed data/app file); 856 features for Minnesota, measured 2026-09-29, ALL of them carrying the Census's city descriptor — there is no village among them, which is why this card has no type row where Michigan's needs one
 NO OFFICEHOLDER. A statutory city elects a mayor and council members, with a clerk and treasurer or a combined clerk-treasurer depending on its plan (Minn. Stat. 412.02, read at revisor.mn.gov on 2026-09-29); a home rule charter city's own charter governs instead. No statewide roster of Minnesota's mayors and council members was found, so the card names nobody. Recorded as the coverage gap mn-municipal-officeholders. Minnesota — statewide, answering only inside an incorporated city.
Post Officepost-office The three nearest post offices to the point, by straight-line distance, with each one's address.
  • USGS The National Map — structures layer 38 (Post Offices), queried live at runtime by a bounding envelope through the same paged loader. Public domain. 1,199 points in Minnesota's envelope, measured 2026-09-29 — 793 in Minnesota, 292 in Wisconsin, 63 in South Dakota, 34 in North Dakota, 10 in Iowa and 7 in Michigan. THE SERVICE LOWERCASES THE ATTRIBUTE KEYS IT RETURNS while naming them in capitals in its own field list, which is why every read goes through the app's case-insensitive findPropCI
 NOT AN OFFICEHOLDER LAYER. A post office elects nobody; the card names buildings and their addresses. Minnesota — statewide, plus points across each border, because nearest is a proximity fact.

About this page

This page is generated from the same list of layers the app itself is built from, so a layer cannot ship without a row here and a row here cannot outlive its layer. If a source above has moved, gone dark, or is simply wrong, that is a bug worth reporting.

Found a source the app is missing? Every accepted one is credited by name in SOURCE_CREDITS.md. There's no cash bounty — the credit is the reward.
