# WATCH.md — Nebraska's watch calendar and deferred measurements

Two things live here: *when to look* for boundary and roster changes in this instance's
sources, and the measurements this instance has taken but not yet acted on. The repo's
`docs/REDISTRICTING_RUNBOOK.md` is *what to do* when a boundary changes. Update the
"Last done" column each time you complete a row — a checkpoint with a stale date is a
checkpoint that didn't happen.

because a weekly job on a boundary would almost always find nothing to change.

| **Annually**, when TIGERweb's AIANNHA service moves to a new vintage | `tribal-areas.json` and `tribal-councils.json` | The Census redraws tribal land rarely, and the land is served cache-first. `python3 scripts/build_tribal_areas.py --check` holds the file to the land-to-government join and the Bureau of Indian Affairs' list on every pull request; it never proves the Census has not redrawn. | **Before each election**, and when an officer changes | `statewide-officials.json` | Built from `data/source/statewide-officials.source.json`, each officer read from their own office's page. The weekly name check in the root goes red on a handover | 2026-11-12 (built) |
| **Annually**, and on any order or act that moves a county between districts | `ne-judicial-districts.json` | Each district is whole counties, read from the district map pages and dissolved from `state-counties.json` by `scripts/build_state_courts.py ne`, which stops unless every county is in exactly one district and there are 12 of them. Re-read with `python3 scripts/build_state_courts.py --fetch ne`, rebuild, and bump `sw.cache_name`, because the file is cache-first. | **Monthly**, and in January 2027 after the judges elected in November take office | `ne-district-judges.json` | Nothing refreshes it yet. Re-read with `python3 scripts/build_state_courts.py --fetch ne`, then rebuild with `python3 scripts/build_state_courts.py ne`; read the excerpt's diff in `ne/data/source/courts/` before committing, because a judge who left shows only as a missing line. The file is network-first, so no cache bump | 2026-11-27 (read: 56 judges named) |
| **Weekly**, by `.github/workflows/watch-city-council-pages.yml`; **monthly** by hand for Omaha; in **mid-December 2026**, after the first-class cities seat the winners of 3 November 2026; and after Omaha's and Lincoln's spring elections in **2027** | `ne-city-councils.json` | Each of the 7 cities lists its own mayor and council and changes the page when a seat changes hands. Bellevue, Grand Island, Kearney, Fremont and Hastings elect in November of even years and seat the winners at the council's first meeting in December (Neb. Rev. Stat. 16-302.01), when Kearney's council also chooses its president, who is the mayor, so every first-class city here needs reading again in December 2026. Omaha and Lincoln elect in the spring of odd years. Kearney's members page prints home addresses and telephones, and neither is ever carried; it names an acting member in place of one who is mobilized, which the card says. The weekly job runs `ne/scripts/build_ne_city_councils.py --watch`, which re-reads every page the seats file cites that this project's reader may read, asking robots.txt first, reports any name or contact no longer on its page and any page it cannot read, and opens or updates the one tracking issue "City council pages need a re-read"; the same command can be run by hand. On a report, read the city's page, change `ne/data/source/ne-city-council-seats.json` by hand (a new member, a changed seat, or the page's own spelling as `as`), and rebuild with the builder, which refuses to write unless every name is on the page it cites. A city that adds or drops a seat changes `SEATS` in the builder; one that starts or stops electing by ward changes the seats' `by` and `WARD_MAPS` in `scripts/build_eam_status.py`. The file is network-first, so no cache bump. **Omaha's council page was read once by hand in Adam's Chrome** (www.cityofomaha.org and citycouncil.cityofomaha.org answer this project's reader with HTTP 403), and the weekly job does not re-read it. Re-read it once a month the same way (stop at a captcha, a check that needs a click or access denied), replace the dated excerpt in `ne/data/source/councils/` and its entries in the builder's `excerpts`, and rebuild. | 2026-11-27 (built: 57 seats in 7 cities, 7 e-mail addresses and 9 numbers, every name found on its page or its dated excerpt) |
| **Quarterly**, and after each decennial census, when the cities redraw their wards | `ne-city-wards.json` | Six cities' maps, each read from the city or its county (Omaha's from the map Douglas County's GIS office keeps for the city; Bellevue's from Sarpy County's election map, asked for Bellevue's alone). A city redraws its wards by ordinance, usually after a census and sometimes after an annexation, and only Omaha's map says when its plan was adopted. Four maps overlap themselves in thin strips along shared lines; the build cuts each strip from one ward and stops if a strip grows wider than the source's `overlap_max_m`. Run `ne/scripts/build_ne_city_wards.py --watch`, which re-reads each map and compares it with the fingerprint recorded when the shipped file was built; on a change, rebuild with the builder, bump `cache_name` because the file is cache-first, and run `ne/scripts/build_ne_city_councils.py --check`, which holds every drawn ward to a seat its voters elect. Kearney elects every seat by the whole city; a charter change that has a ward's own voters elect its member would add that city here | 2026-11-28 (built: 30 wards and districts in 6 cities) |
