Sources & Data Layers Every layer in districtry Wisconsin, and exactly where its data comes from.

← Back to the map 🧩 What data is missing?

This app is a reader over other people's data. It draws no boundary of its own and names no officeholder it cannot cite, so the useful question about any answer it gives is who published this, and when. That is what this page is for: the publishers the app credits as a whole, then one row per layer naming the source behind that layer specifically.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Always confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.

Data last verified: September 11, 2026

Where a source publishes nothing, the app says so rather than guessing — the missing-data panel on the map is the standing list of what nobody publishes yet, and what would close each gap.

Who publishes the data

The upstream publishers this app depends on, across all layers. Per-layer provenance is in the matrix below.

  • OpenStreetMap contributors (Nominatim & Photon/Komoot) Address search and the office-address pins.
  • U.S. Census TIGERweb The national boundary service behind every starter layer: counties, county subdivisions, municipalities, school districts, and the congressional districts.
  • unitedstates/congress-legislators The public-domain roster of sitting members of Congress, including district and D.C. offices.
  • Open States The sourced, machine-maintained roster of sitting Wisconsin legislators — both chambers' names, parties, official pages and offices (wi.csv current-people export).
  • USGS The National Map The public-domain structures layer behind the nearest-post-office list.
  • Wisconsin Legislative Technology Services Bureau (LTSB) The statewide county board supervisory districts — LTSB's open aggregate of every county's twice-yearly boundary filing under Wis. Stat. 5.15(4)(br)1.
  • Trempealeau County The county's own adopted 2021-2031 supervisory district plan, shipped in place of LTSB's file for that county alone.

The layer matrix

31 layers — 7 political and legislative, 8 public safety, 6 schools, 10 geography and amenities. Each row names the publisher the boundary comes from, where the names on the card come from, and the ground the layer actually answers on — outside that ground the layer hides itself rather than guessing.

It is the only source in Wisconsin that names them as data, and its own last data edit predates the April 2026 election that fills these offices, so a NAME is shown only where the municipality's own page witnessed it at build time; where it could not be witnessed the card names the office and says why the person is not shown. STATEWIDE, and in all 608 cities and villages, the card also names the municipal CLERK — a different officer, labelled separately, from the Wisconsin Elections Commission's own clerk directory (the Commission sent it on request; it also publishes it at elections.wi.gov/clerks/directory, which refuses automation). Each clerk row carries the Commission's OWN per-record last-updated date, which runs from 2017 to 2026, and the card states that date rather than implying the whole directory is current. The directory carries NO e-mail address — the clerks asked the Commission to withhold them — and this project ships NO address from it, because the file cannot tell a village hall from a clerk's own house and demonstrably contains both. STATEWIDE it is the Wisconsin Elections Commission's own list, which the Commission sent on request after its website's Cloudflare challenge had been recorded as a block — a challenge on a website is not a refusal by the agency behind it (wi/scripts/build_wi_polling_places.py, 3,623 reporting units, 2,475 places, shipped as 72 per-county files; 7,131 of LTSB's 7,161 wards paired, 99.58%). That list is PROVISIONAL until the Commission publishes its November edition, and the card says so in the reader's own sentence, names the election, dates the pull, and RETIRES the pairing once the election date has passed — a per-election list that outlives its election looks current and is not. The 29 wards in 12 municipalities the Commission's file omits fall back to the MyVote lookup and are the Data gaps panel's `ward-polling-places` record. THE TWO CITIES THAT PUBLISH THEIR OWN PAIRING STAY FIRST, because the municipality runs the election. INSIDE MILWAUKEE (phase 4 PR 3): the city publishes its own ward-to-polling-place dataset (CC-BY), whose ward numbers are gated equal to LTSB's Milwaukee wards and whose every pair is witnessed against the city's own REST layer (wi/scripts/build_mke_polling_places.py, mke-polling-places.json) — name, address, the city's entrance and parking guidance, and the dataset edition stated on the card, with MyVote linked as the confirmation route because polling places move between elections. MADISON is the second city: its open-data server publishes the pairing as ONE layer — a polling point per ward, 137 rows — whose ward keys are gated equal to LTSB's Madison wards AND the city's own ward layer (wi/scripts/build_madison_polling_places.py, madison-polling-places.json); the layer publishes no edition date, so each build dates the pairing as the day it was read. About this page

This page is generated from the same list of layers the app itself is built from, so a layer cannot ship without a row here and a row here cannot outlive its layer. If a source above has moved, gone dark, or is simply wrong, that is a bug worth reporting.

Found a source the app is missing? Every accepted one is credited by name in SOURCE_CREDITS.md. There's no cash bounty — the credit is the reward.
