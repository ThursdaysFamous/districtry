What township am I in?

Illinois township lookup — free, by address, ZIP, or a tap on the map.

A township is the layer of Illinois government most people never look up, and it is the one that assesses your house. Outside Chicago, township government runs property assessment, general assistance and — in most townships — the roads that no city or county maintains. This names the 220 officials the Cook County Clerk publishes for its 29 townships.

What the lookup shows

Select any point and the card names the township containing it and, where the county publishes them, the supervisor, clerk, assessor, highway commissioner and trustees who hold its offices.

The map draws a township for any point in Illinois, because the boundaries come from the Census Bureau's county-subdivision layer for the whole state. The names do not: they are published county by county, and today Cook County's clerk is the one publishing them. A township with no entry shows its name and nobody else's.

Township, city and county are three different governments

They overlap and none contains the others. A township can hold several municipalities and parts of others; Chicago sits in no township at all, which is why the card is blank inside the city. Your property assessment comes from the township assessor, your street repair from whichever of the three actually owns the road, and your ballot carries all three.

Where the names come from

The Cook County Clerk's directory of elected officials, re-read on a schedule and landing as a reviewed pull request. A township the directory does not name keeps its identity card and nobody is filled in.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.
