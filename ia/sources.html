Sources & Data Layers Every layer in districtry Iowa, and exactly where its data comes from.

← Back to the map 🧩 What data is missing?

This app is a reader over other people's data. It draws no boundary of its own and names no officeholder it cannot cite, so the useful question about any answer it gives is who published this, and when. That is what this page is for: the publishers the app credits as a whole, then one row per layer naming the source behind that layer specifically.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Always confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.

Data last verified: August 27, 2026

Where a source publishes nothing, the app says so rather than guessing — the missing-data panel on the map is the standing list of what nobody publishes yet, and what would close each gap.

Who publishes the data

The upstream publishers this app depends on, across all layers. Per-layer provenance is in the matrix below.

  • U.S. Census Bureau TIGERweb County and legislative-district boundaries.
  • unitedstates/congress-legislators U.S. House roster (CC0).
  • Open States Iowa Senate and House roster base.
  • Iowa Legislature Senate and House office enrichment.

Every other county names nobody here on purpose — under plan 1 there are no districts and under plan 2 supervisors are elected countywide, so a name against a district would assert an election that did not happen. NO PHONE RIDES A SUPERVISOR'S ROW: all 17 publish one number for the whole board, which is the board office and renders as such. ia-county-board-directory.json (board size read back from the shipped geometry + the county's own official site from the Iowa State Association of Counties' member directory) supplies that link; Jones County carries no row in either file — it is entirely absent from the state's own aggregate (measured by name and by FIPS), a recorded gap rather than a guess. ia-county-board-chairs.json then marks WHICH supervisor chairs the board, in 40 of 99 counties, from each county's own board page and — for five of the largest, whose pages never use the word — from the county's own board minutes: no statewide publisher records it — the association directory that supplies the members carries no chair field, and the auditors' association publishes auditors, who are not board members — and the chair is chosen by the board's own January vote, so it rides a weekly re-scrape rather than a one-time answer. The card joins it BY NAME, so a chair who leaves the board stops rendering rather than sliding onto a successor. Iowa — 98 of 99 counties (Jones County is a recorded gap).

The one real artifact is recorded rather than smoothed — wards 1 and 2 overlap along 2.6 km of their shared edge by a ribbon averaging 14 m wide, 9.3 acres in all, Polsby-Popper compactness 0.0169.
  • City of Waterloo — its own Wards feature service, pre-built and shipped with the app (5 polygons, services1.arcgis.com/QOAXA4I2iTKKdBuy). Queried unauthenticated 2026-09-04: capabilities Query,Extract, and BOTH serviceDescription AND copyrightText EMPTY. That emptiness is recorded rather than read as permission or as refusal — it is the plain difference from Des Moines, whose terms REQUIRE a verbatim disclaimer, and it is why Waterloo's card carries no data notice.
  • City of Waterloo — its own City Limits layer, read at BUILD TIME only, to prove the five wards tile the city: erasing them leaves 0.0216% of its 62.98 sq mi uncovered in 156 fragments, largest 7,909 m². That largest fragment is BIGGER than Des Moines's 3,482 m² and is not a worse result: its bounding box is 36 m by 3,156 m at Polsby-Popper 0.0025, a ribbon three kilometres long and a lane wide. NOT ONE of the 156 fragments scores above 0.30 (median 0.0016), so the builder gates on SHAPE as well as size — a genuine hole is one COMPACT part, and an area-only ceiling copied across from Des Moines would eventually admit one.
  • Linn County — its own ElectionsCityCouncilDistrict service, pre-built and shipped with the app. NOT the city's layer: it is the COUNTY's and it holds NINE polygons for the two Linn cities that elect by district, Cedar Rapids's five and Marion's four, separated only by POLITICAL_TWP — a bare code ('27' and '21') with no name field, no domain and no description anywhere in the service. Queried unauthenticated 2026-09-04: copyrightText and serviceDescription both EMPTY, so no notice ships. A BUILD KEYED ON AN OPAQUE CODE MUST PROVE THE CODE: nothing published says 27 is Cedar Rapids, and a single downtown point test would still look right if the county swapped the two codes.
  • Linn County — "Cedar Rapids Council Districts & Marion Ward Maps", the county's own election-services page, publishing five Cedar Rapids district PDFs beside a Marion wards map. It is what the CITY of Cedar Rapids' own "Find Your District" page sends readers to, which is the city naming the county as the authority for its own district lines — the provenance link this layer rides in place of an in-band roster.
  • TIGERweb incorporated places, read at BUILD TIME only, to prove the five districts tile Cedar Rapids AND that POLITICAL_TWP=27 is the city it claims. Linn publishes no city-limits polygon at all (its RealEstateBoundary is a cadastral LINE layer), so this is a CROSS-PUBLISHER comparison where Des Moines's and Waterloo's are same-publisher — which is why every ceiling is looser: residual 0.1338% of 75.54 sq mi in 366 fragments (largest 34,190 m²) against Waterloo's 0.0216%. None of that difference is a worse boundary; it is two organisations digitising one city in different years. The compactness floor is raised from 200 m² to 2,000 m² for the same reason — the residual's two most compact fragments are 264 m² at Polsby-Popper 0.617 and 172 m² at 0.461, corner slivers where two outlines disagree, and 2,000 m² is the scale at which a compact fragment could hold somebody. ZERO fragments are both above that floor and above 0.30. THE IDENTITY IS RE-PROVEN EVERY RUN IN BOTH DIRECTIONS: 27 must tile Cedar Rapids (area ratio 1.00323) and 21 must NOT (it leaves 99.996% of the city uncovered). The second is the gate that fires if the county ever reassigns the codes.
 All seven seats Des Moines elects, from the city's own council page (dsm-council-members.json, weekly CI): the four ward members keyed to their wards, and the mayor plus both at-large members in a citywide block the card renders under its own heading — Iowa Code §372.4(1)(b) seats a mayor, two at-large members and one member from each of four wards, so a ward layer alone would answer four sevenths of the question and look complete doing it (§372.4(2): the mayor is not a member of the council, and is badged Mayor rather than folded into a council list). Name, phone, e-mail, election date and term expiry ship for all seven. THE BOUNDARY'S OWN IN-BAND ROSTER IS NOT READ: the Wards service carries a member name and e-mail on each polygon and they are correct today, but a roster attached to a boundary is refreshed when the boundary is — ward 2's feature still carried its 2024-02-16 edit while wards 1, 3 and 4 were edited across 2025-12-29..31 for the November 2025 cycle — and it has no phone field at all. It is used ONCE, at build time, as a WITNESS: the builder refuses to write a roster whose four ward members disagree with the polygons' own names and e-mails. The city publishes seven DISTINCT phone numbers, so the fleet's switchboard test is re-run every build and correctly does not fire. WATERLOO'S SEVEN COUNCIL SEATS come from the city's own council page the same way (waterloo-council-members.json, weekly CI): five ward members keyed to their wards, both at-large members in the citywide block, each with a phone, an e-mail and a term-expiry date. ITS MAYOR IS DELIBERATELY ABSENT — he is elected citywide, and the at-large rule puts a citywide officer on the unit's identity card rather than a district card; the card names him in prose so the council does not read as the whole of the city's elected government. Des Moines carries its mayor in citywide for historical reasons and that is left alone. THE BOUNDARY'S OWN IN-BAND ROSTER IS NOT READ HERE EITHER: the Wards service carries Ward_Councilperson and both at-large columns and they are correct today (its dataLastEditDate was one day old when measured), but it has no phone, no e-mail and no term, and a roster attached to a boundary is refreshed when the boundary is. It is used ONCE, at build time, as a WITNESS: all five wards and both at-large members must agree, matched on first+last token because the page prints "Hector A. Salamanca-Arroyo" where the polygon carries "Hector Salamanca-Arroyo" — and that match is PRINTED every run so a normalisation cannot hide a real difference. The council page also repeats every member in a bio-link anchor, TWO of which disagree with the member's own line ("Steve Simons" for Steve Simon), so the scraper keys on the term-expiry line and keeps the anchors as its control. CEDAR RAPIDS SHIPS EIGHT OF ITS NINE SEATS (cedar-rapids-council-members.json, weekly CI): five district members keyed to their districts and three at-large members in the citywide block, each with a phone, an e-mail and a term-expiry year, from the city's own seven seat pages. ITS MAYOR IS SCRAPED AND DELIBERATELY NOT SHIPPED — the at-large rule again — and scraping her is what makes that a decision the pipeline can demonstrate rather than a gap it cannot see; the scraper demands all nine seats, so the day her page reshapes it fails instead of quietly shipping eight. THERE IS NO IN-BAND ROSTER TO WITNESS AGAINST AND NONE IS FAKED: Linn's layer carries POLITICAL_TWP, CITYCOUNCIL and Updated, and no names at all. What is witnessed is the NUMBERING, against the shipped boundary — the five districts the roster names must be exactly the five the shipped file draws — which catches a district drawn with no member and a member keyed to no district, and will NOT catch a stale name. That is weaker than Des Moines's and Waterloo's witnesses and is recorded as weaker. Two traps are gated rather than described: 319-286-5763 is the City Clerk's number and sits in the furniture of ALL SEVEN pages, so contact is read only from inside each member's own <p> and the builder refuses if that number ever appears inside a member block; and the council seats TWO OLSONS (Tyler at-large, Scott in District 4), so surname is not a key and a repeated FULL name fails the scrape. One of the eight addresses is off the city's domain — the city publishes scott@scotteolson.com, his own firm — and it ships, witnessed by name under the county officers' address test applied unchanged, because consulting the domain errs in both directions. The City of Des Moines, the City of Waterloo and the City of Cedar Rapids only — each city's own wards or districts are its entry's coverage test, all three measured to tile their city (above). Iowa's other 936 cities elect councils this instance does not yet name by district; the City card answers identity for them, and names officials in five of them.

About this page

This page is generated from the same list of layers the app itself is built from, so a layer cannot ship without a row here and a row here cannot outlive its layer. If a source above has moved, gone dark, or is simply wrong, that is a bug worth reporting.

Found a source the app is missing? Every accepted one is credited by name in SOURCE_CREDITS.md. There's no cash bounty — the credit is the reward.
