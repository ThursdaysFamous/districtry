# WATCH.md — redistricting watch calendar

The one place the dates live: *when to look* for boundary and roster changes in this
instance's sources. The repo's `docs/REDISTRICTING_RUNBOOK.md` is *what to do* when a
boundary changes. Update the "Last done" column each time you complete a row — a
checkpoint with a stale date is a checkpoint that didn't happen.

This instance arrived on 2026-09-29 with eleven layers, every one of them from a national
publisher, and exactly one roster. So this file is short on purpose: most of what a mature
instance watches here is a county or city publisher, and Indiana has none of those yet.

## Open questions this instance owes an answer to

| Question | Why it is open | What would close it |
|---|---|---|
| **Who runs an Indiana county, and on which of its two maps?** | An Indiana county is run by TWO elected bodies drawn on their OWN district lines — a three-member board of commissioners (IC 36-2-2) and a seven-member county council (IC 36-2-3, four districted seats and three at large in most counties). Neither can ride the other's geometry and neither can ride the county outline, so this instance draws no county body at all and the county card says so in those words. It is the largest recorded gap in the instance. Gap `in-county-government` | A per-county boundary source for one or both bodies, county by county, the way Illinois grew its `county-board` dispatcher — plus the officeholder sourcing in the SAME change (`docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` Part 2 rule 4). The at-large council seats never get a polygon; where a county elects its commissioners at large there is nothing to draw and only a record to correct. Indiana has 92 counties, so the first question is whether the state publishes one layer for all of them (the Michigan shape) or nothing at all (the Illinois shape). NOT YET ASKED OF ANY PUBLISHER. |
| **Who are Indiana's 150 state legislators?** | Neither chamber card names a person. The General Assembly publishes a legislator directory at `iga.in.gov/information/legislators`, which both cards link, and this instance ships no roster read from it or from anywhere else. Gap `in-general-assembly-roster` | A roster keyed by district number — the chamber factory already joins one on sight, so this is a scraper + builder pair and no app change (`ia/scripts/` and `mi/scripts/` both carry the shape, Iowa's from a state feed and Michigan's from Open States plus a chamber directory). Check Open States (`data.openstates.org/people/current/in.csv`) first, because it is one fetch and covers both chambers; measure what it carries per field before wiring it, since Michigan's answer had zero capitol phones. Its robots.txt was already read on 2026-09-29 through `scripts/robots_policy.py` as the client that would crawl it: the host serves HTML at that path rather than a policy, so `*` binds nothing, there is no `Crawl-delay` and no `Content-Signal` — re-read it before the first fetch anyway, since that measurement is four months old by the time anybody acts on it. |
| **Neither chamber card can state its own gap, and the county card can.** | The county card's render is this instance's own code, so it says plainly that it names nobody and why. The chamber cards go through the shared `registerIlgaChamber` fence, whose roster-miss path renders the district number and the directory link and nothing else — which is honest but silent. Here that path is what EVERY click shows, not an exception, and the gaps panel is the only place the reason is stated | Either the General Assembly roster (which retires the question) or an engine change giving the factory an optional roster-miss note. The second is a fence edit and therefore a fleet change: it would have to be right for every instance, including the ones where a roster miss really is the exception. Do not solve it with an instance-side copy of the factory. |
| **Nobody is named on the township, municipal or school cards either, and those are three separate absences.** | The Census carries geography and no officeholder field at all, so these are not sources that went quiet — they never had a name to give. Indiana townships elect a trustee and a three-member board; cities elect a mayor and a common council (IC 36-4) and towns a town council and no mayor (IC 36-5); school boards are elected per district. Gaps `in-township-officers`, `in-municipal-officeholders`, `in-school-board-members` | A publisher for each, per body, and the honest answer may be that none exists statewide. Indiana has 1,004 civil townships and 566 cities and towns, so a statewide roster is the only route worth taking — a per-unit scrape at that scale is not one. |
| **Indiana's bbox clears Chicago's centre by 0.117° and no gate says so out loud.** | `validate_index.py` fails any instance whose `metro_bbox` contains a sibling metro's centre. Indiana's `maxLat` is 41.77 and Chicago's centre is 41.8781, so the rule passes — by 0.117° of latitude, about 13 km. That is the narrowest margin in the fleet and it is a property of the state's own extent, not of a choice made here. A future northern-Indiana layer that widened the box to include Lake Michigan water would fail that gate | Nothing needs doing. It is recorded so that a later widening is read as the cause rather than as a mysterious gate failure, and so nobody "fixes" it by relaxing the rule. Selections are already decided by state OUTLINES rather than by this rectangle (ENGINE `metro-portal` + `fleet-outlines.json`), and the smoke test's 1a2 asserts all three branches from here — Danville to Illinois, Niles to Michigan, Louisville to nowhere. |
| **Indiana is DARK: it is in no `metros.json`, no `fleet-outlines.json`, no sitemap and no landing card.** | Go-live is its own change (`docs/EXPANSION_GUIDE.md` §2.4 and the `new-state-instance` skill §11), and `.github/workflows/deploy-pages.yml` excludes `in/**` wholesale until then | The go-live PR: narrow that exclude to the granular set the other instances use, add `in` to the deploy's presence loop, add the `metros.json` entry and run `--sync-fleet`, add `in` to `AREAS` in `scripts/build_coverage_map.py`, list every page in `sitemap.xml`, add the README row and bump its instance count, then regenerate the landing page, the privacy page, the coverage map and `fleet-outlines.json`. Until then this instance is not in `fleet-outlines.json`, so a sibling app cannot hand a reader HERE — only the other way round. |

## Per-decade — the census redistricting cycle

| When | What | Last done |
|---|---|---|
| Whenever TIGERweb rolls a vintage | **The congressional layer's district field is versioned and the old one is REMOVED, not merely stale.** It is `CD120` for the 120th Congress and a query naming the retired `CD119` is rejected with HTTP 400 rather than returning nothing. `in/scripts/build_legislative_boundaries.py` names `CD120`; the app's `CONGRESS_DISTRICT_FIELDS` lists it first. On the next roll BOTH need the new name | 2026-09-29 (CD120) |
| Whenever TIGERweb rolls a vintage | **The seven live layers rebuild themselves — what a roll can break is silent.** (1) `zip-code` is fetched by an Esri `{xmin,ymin,xmax,ymax}` ENVELOPE rather than `STATE='18'`, because ZCTAs carry no STATE field; passing the instance's `{minLng,...}` shape instead yields HTTP 200 with a JSON error envelope and zero features rather than an error, which is how `ia/` shipped a dead ZIP overlay for weeks with a working card. `validate_sources.py` watches that count with a floor. (2) The Township or City and City or Town cards derive their type row from TIGER's own NAME-minus-BASENAME suffix, and an unseen suffix renders NO type row rather than a wrong one — safe and invisible — so re-measure the distribution. Measured 2026-09-29: subdivisions 44 township 1,004 · 43 town 4 · 00 blank 2 · 46 UT 1 · 25 city 1; places 43 town 446 · 25 city 119 · 00 blank 1. (3) **Indiana runs UNIFIED school districts alone**: TIGERweb School layer 1 (secondary) and layer 2 (elementary) both answer ZERO for `STATE='18'`, so the one shipped layer tiles the state and neither sibling is watched — there is no layer for them to break. If either stops measuring zero, the recorded drop becomes a layer to ship, and the unified card's copy stops being complete | 2026-09-29 (measured at the arrival build) |
| After each decennial census (P.L. 94-171 release, spring of the year after) | Every boundary this instance ships is redrawn. Indiana's congressional and legislative maps are drawn by the **General Assembly** itself (IC 3-3-2 / IC 2-1-1), not by a commission, and enacted as ordinary statute — so the signal is a redistricting bill rather than a commission calendar. Rebuild all three chamber files and the county file, then re-anchor the smoke test | 2026-09-29 (built against the 2020-cycle maps: 9 congressional, 50 Senate, 100 House) |
| At each rebuild of the chamber files | **Indiana's chambers DO NOT NEST, and a future builder must not assume they do.** 100 House districts over 50 Senate districts reads like Illinois's exact 2-to-1 rule and is not one: measured 2026-09-29 at every House district's own TIGER interior point, the House districts per Senate district run 1×11, 2×24, 3×11, 4×2, and the numbering is unrelated (Senate 1 holds House 12 and 15). That is why `in/scripts/build_legislative_boundaries.py` carries no nesting gate — one here could only ever be vacuous — and asserts a complete self-consistent partition of each chamber instead. Re-measure after a redistricting rather than copying Illinois's check across | 2026-09-29 |
