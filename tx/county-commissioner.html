Who are my county commissioners?

Texas county judges and county commissioners — free, by address, ZIP, or a tap on the map.

← Back to the map

A Texas county is governed by its commissioners court: the county judge, who presides and is elected by the whole county, and four commissioners, each elected from one of the county's four commissioner precincts. The lookup finds your county and names its county judge and, in the counties listed below, its four commissioners.

Or leave it blank to open the map and pick your location there.

What the lookup shows

Select any point in Texas and the county card names the county, then its county judge, and then its four commissioners wherever the county's own website is read.

The map does not draw commissioner precincts yet, so the card names all four commissioners and cannot say which one represents the point you picked. Your county's website or its elections office can tell you which precinct you live in.

Where the names come from, and what they are not

No state office publishes a list of Texas's county commissioners, so each county's are read from the county's own website, once a week. The county pages below cover the counties read so far. The rest are not listed yet: their websites turn automated readers away, ask them not to read the page, or do not print the commissioners' names in a way a program can read reliably. The map's county card still names their county judge.

The county judge's name comes from the Texas Office of Court Administration's judicial directory, which lists the judges of every court in the state, county by county. Each page prints the date of the directory file it was read from. Every county judge's term ends on 31 December 2026, so from 1 January 2027 a page can name the outgoing judge until the office publishes a new file.

A county's website is the county's own word, but it can lag an election or a resignation. Where a county's page says a seat is vacant, its page here says so too.

Where the boundaries come from

The county lines come from the U.S. Census Bureau. Each county draws its own commissioner precincts, and the map does not draw them yet. Every source is on the sources & data layers page.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and officeholder data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Confirm officeholders with your county before relying on them for anything official.
