Who is my NH legislator?

New Hampshire General Court district lookup — free, by address, ZIP, or a tap on the map.

← Back to the map

The New Hampshire General Court has 24 Senate districts and 203 House districts. The two chambers are drawn separately and a House district can elect several representatives, so the only way to know which districts and members cover an address is to put the address on the map. Senators and representatives both serve two-year terms.

Or leave it blank to open the map and pick your location there.

Find your New Hampshire General Court districts →

Opens the map with both chambers on. Search your address or ZIP, or tap your location.

What the lookup shows

Select any point and the two cards name its Senate district and senator, and its House district with every representative it elects, including the floterial district drawn over it where there is one.

Every other district covering the same point is listed beside them, so one click answers the state question and the county, city and federal ones together.

Two chambers, and floterial districts

An address has one Senate district and one or two House districts. Each House district is named for a county and numbered within it. Most of them cover the state between them; the rest are floterial districts, each drawn over two or more of those and electing more representatives from all of them together, so a reader in a district that belongs to a floterial is represented by the members of both. The tables below list every district in full, each floterial with the districts it is drawn over, so a reader who knows their district can look up the members without opening the map at all.

Where the names come from

Both rosters are re-read on a schedule from the legislature's own published member lists and land as a reviewed pull request; the map names no officeholder it cannot cite. A seat the source does not name is shown as unnamed rather than filled in.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.
