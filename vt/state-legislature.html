Who is my Vermont legislator?

Vermont General Assembly district lookup — free, by address, ZIP, or a tap on the map.

← Back to the map

The Vermont General Assembly has 16 Senate districts and 109 House districts. Each district is named for the county or counties it lies in rather than numbered, many elect more than one member, and the two chambers are drawn separately, so the only way to know which districts cover an address is to put the address on the map. Senators and representatives both serve two-year terms.

Or leave it blank to open the map and pick your location there.

Find your Vermont General Assembly districts →

Opens the map with both chambers on. Search your address or ZIP, or tap your location.

What the lookup shows

Select any point and the two cards name the district for each chamber and every member it elects, with their party and a link to their own page on the Legislature's site.

Every other district covering the same point is listed beside them, so one click answers the state question and the county, city and federal ones together.

Two chambers, named districts

An address has one district in each chamber. A Senate district can elect more than one senator and a House district more than one representative, so a reader is represented by every member their two districts elect. The tables below list both chambers in full, district by district, so a reader who knows their districts can look up the members without opening the map at all.

Where the names come from

Both rosters are re-read on a schedule from the legislature's own published member lists and land as a reviewed pull request; the map names no officeholder it cannot cite. A seat the source does not name is shown as unnamed rather than filled in.

Not for legal or official use. Boundary and roster data come from public sources that explicitly disclaim legal precision. Confirm district assignments and officeholders with the relevant government office before relying on them for anything official.
